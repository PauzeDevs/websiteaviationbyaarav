United to debut private‑jet‑styled CRJ450 regional jets in December

United Airlines will introduce a reconfigured CRJ200, dubbed the CRJ450, featuring a two‑class cabin and enhanced luggage storage, with initial service from Chicago and Denver slated for late 2026.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-11 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

United Airlines plans to roll out its first CRJ450 aircraft—a refurbished Bombardier CRJ200 with a premium cabin layout—in December 2026. Operated by SkyWest Airlines under the United Express brand, the 41‑seat jet will initially serve Chicago O'Hare and Denver International airports. The conversion reduces capacity, adds a first‑class section, and redesigns overhead storage, aiming to improve the passenger experience on routes that cannot sustain larger regional jets. The information comes from Simple Flying, which cites reporting from The Points Guy and confirmation from Aviation Week, but no independent verification is available.

Background and strategic intent

United has announced a new regional‑jet offering that repurposes the existing CRJ200 airframe rather than introducing a fresh type. The move is presented as a way to upgrade service on short‑haul routes while preserving the operational flexibility of a smaller aircraft. According to Simple Flying, the initiative aligns with United’s broader effort to enhance its United Express product without relying solely on larger regional jets.

Conversion specifics

The first CRJ450, registered N973SW, is a modified CRJ200 that reduces the standard 50‑seat layout to 41 seats. The cabin is divided into three zones: seven United First seats, sixteen Economy Plus seats, and eighteen standard economy seats. Overhead bins in the first‑class area have been replaced with a dedicated closet, creating more open space around the seats. In economy, the bins have been enlarged to accommodate standard roll‑aboard bags, a change intended to reduce the need for passengers to check larger carry‑ons on regional flights. The aircraft will also feature onboard Wi‑Fi and expanded luggage storage.

Operational rollout

United expects the inaugural CRJ450 to enter service in December 2026, operating from Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD) and Denver International Airport (DEN). The aircraft will be operated by SkyWest Airlines on United Express flights. Simple Flying notes that Aviation Week confirmed the first two conversions were still in progress as of early November. While United has not released a full destination list, the article mentions Lafayette, Indiana, as a potential market, though it clarifies that this should not be taken as a confirmed route.

Market and passenger impact

By offering a two‑class product on a 41‑seat regional jet, United aims to provide a higher‑end experience on routes that traditionally rely on all‑economy configurations. The redesign addresses common complaints about limited overhead storage on small jets and introduces a premium cabin that could attract business travelers on short hops. Analysts cited by Simple Flying suggest the approach could make smaller communities more attractive for United Express service, but the actual commercial effect remains untested pending the aircraft’s entry into service.

Key facts

  • United’s first CRJ450 will be a refurbished CRJ200 with 41 seats, including a dedicated first‑class cabin
  • Initial service is planned for December 2026 from Chicago O'Hare and Denver International airports
  • The conversion replaces first‑class overhead bins with a closet and enlarges economy bins for standard roll‑aboard bags
  • SkyWest Airlines will operate the aircraft under the United Express brand
  • Details on the full route network have not been disclosed

What this means

The CRJ450 conversion reflects United’s strategy to differentiate its regional offering without the capital outlay of a new aircraft type. By reducing seat count and adding a premium cabin, United can market a higher‑value product on routes that lack sufficient demand for larger jets. The luggage‑storage redesign directly tackles a frequent pain point for regional‑jet passengers, potentially improving customer satisfaction and loyalty. However, the success of this model will depend on passenger uptake of the premium seats and the airline’s ability to fill the reduced capacity profitably. If the aircraft proves popular, United may consider extending the conversion program to additional CRJ200s, but the current plan remains limited to the first two conversions.
