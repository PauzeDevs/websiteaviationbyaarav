Qantas opts for proven Rolls‑Royce powerplant on A350‑1000ULR for Project Sunrise

The Australian carrier selected the Airbus A350‑1000ULR equipped with a Rolls‑Royce engine for its ultra‑long‑haul Sydney‑London and Sydney‑New York routes, favoring reliability over newer technology, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-18 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Qantas announced that its Project Sunrise will be launched with the Airbus A350‑1000ULR powered by a Rolls‑Royce engine, rejecting the Boeing 777X despite its advanced engines. The decision reflects a strategic emphasis on proven performance, fuel efficiency and operational certainty for flights exceeding 20 hours. The first aircraft is slated for delivery in April 2027, with the Sydney‑London service expected to begin in October 2027.

Project Sunrise: ultra‑long‑haul ambition

Qantas’ Project Sunrise aims to introduce nonstop services that surpass today’s longest commercial flights. The carrier plans to connect Sydney with London Heathrow and New York JFK, routes that will cover roughly 9,200 nautical miles and require more than 20 hours in the air. The initiative follows a decade of growth in ultra‑long‑haul traffic, driven by improvements in aircraft efficiency and passenger demand for direct connections.

Aircraft selection: A350‑1000ULR over 777X

In 2022 Qantas placed an order for the Airbus A350‑1000ULR after a competitive evaluation that also considered Boeing’s 777X. Although the 777X is equipped with the latest generation of jet engines, Qantas chose the older A350 platform, citing its established long‑range capabilities and the airline’s confidence in the type’s operational record. The decision positions the A350‑1000ULR as the flagship for the longest routes in the airline’s fleet.

Engine choice: reliability prioritized

The Simple Flying report notes that Qantas selected a Rolls‑Royce engine for the A350‑1000ULR, describing it as a "proven" powerplant. While the article does not specify the exact engine model, the emphasis is on the engine’s demonstrated service history rather than theoretical performance gains offered by newer designs. Qantas appears to value the predictability of maintenance, fuel burn and dispatch reliability for flights that push the limits of endurance.

Timeline and network impact

According to the source, the first A350‑1000ULR will arrive in April 2027 for crew familiarisation flights. The Sydney‑London service is expected to launch in October 2027 once three aircraft are in service. A subsequent Sydney‑New York route will follow, and the airline plans to integrate the ULR variant across its network, potentially replacing Boeing 787s on existing long‑haul routes. While Qantas has hinted at future Melbourne‑London and Melbourne‑New York services, these appear lower priority at present.

Key facts

  • Project Sunrise will operate nonstop Sydney‑London and Sydney‑New York flights exceeding 20 hours
  • Qantas selected the Airbus A350‑1000ULR instead of the Boeing 777X
  • The aircraft will be powered by a Rolls‑Royce engine deemed "proven" by the airline
  • First A350‑1000ULR delivery scheduled for April 2027; Sydney‑London launch slated for October 2027

What this means

Qantas’ choice reflects a risk‑averse strategy that favours established technology for a venture where operational certainty is paramount. By pairing the A350‑1000ULR with a Rolls‑Royce engine that has a long service record, the airline mitigates potential reliability issues that could arise on flights lasting more than 20 hours. The decision also underscores the competitive advantage Airbus gains in the ultra‑long‑haul niche, as the A350‑1000ULR’s range and fuel efficiency align with Qantas’ goals without requiring the newer, less‑tested engine options that Boeing promoted for the 777X.
