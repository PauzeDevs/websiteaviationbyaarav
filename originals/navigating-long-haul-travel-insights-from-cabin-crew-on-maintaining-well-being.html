Navigating Long-Haul Travel: Insights from Cabin Crew on Maintaining Well-being

Seasoned flight attendants share strategies for managing the physical challenges of extended air travel, with a primary focus on hydration.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-08 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

A report on practical advice from experienced cabin crew members regarding the management of physical discomfort and dehydration during long-haul flights.

The Challenge of Cabin Environments

Long-haul air travel presents unique physiological challenges for passengers, primarily due to the cabin environment. According to Simple Flying, the air inside an aircraft typically maintains a humidity level between 10% and 20%, a condition described as being drier than a desert. This environment contributes to rapid fluid loss through breathing and sitting, which can lead to fatigue, headaches, and dry skin.

Hydration Strategies for Passengers

Cabin crew members emphasize that hydration is the most critical factor in mitigating the effects of long-duration flights. Air New Zealand flight attendant Anne-Marie Miller suggests that passengers should consume eight glasses of water prior to boarding and continue to sip water throughout the journey to ensure they arrive feeling refreshed. The Aerospace Medical Association (AsMA) supports this approach, recommending an intake of approximately 0.25 liters (8 ounces) of water per hour of flight time to offset the estimated 236–250 ml of fluid lost by the human body every hour at cruising altitude.

Practical Access to Water

While airlines typically provide water during cabin service, flight attendants suggest that passengers take proactive steps to manage their own hydration. American Airlines flight attendant Larry Holcombe recommends that travelers carry a refillable water bottle. This allows passengers to request refills from the crew during service or at other times, and provides a cost-effective alternative to purchasing bottled water at airport terminals. Passengers are encouraged to utilize airport drinking fountains to fill their containers before boarding.

Key facts

  • Cabin humidity levels on aircraft typically range between 10% and 20%.
  • The human body loses approximately 8 ounces of water per hour at cruising altitude.
  • The Aerospace Medical Association recommends drinking 8 ounces of water per hour of flight.
  • Flight attendants suggest using refillable water bottles to ensure consistent access to hydration.

What this means

The advice provided by cabin crew aligns with established aerospace medical guidelines regarding fluid loss. The emphasis on self-sufficiency—specifically the use of refillable bottles—suggests that while airlines provide service, the responsibility for maintaining adequate hydration levels during long-haul segments rests largely with the passenger's proactive management.
