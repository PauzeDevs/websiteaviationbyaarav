United and American Airlines Clash Over Gate Allocation at Chicago O’Hare

A rivalry over gate assignments, schedule expansions and federal limits has turned O’Hare into a contested arena for the two legacy carriers, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-22 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

United Airlines and American Airlines are engaged in an escalating dispute over gate space and flight slots at Chicago O’Hare International Airport. The conflict, described by Simple Flying, involves lobbying, legal action, aggressive schedule growth, and intervention by the Federal Aviation Administration. The outcome could reshape the competitive dynamics at one of the United States’ busiest hubs.

O’Hare’s Gate Allocation Framework

Chicago O’Hare’s gate distribution is governed by a city‑run use‑and‑lease system rather than the FAA’s slot regime that controls airports such as New York’s JFK. Airlines receive preferential‑use gates based on their departure share and historical flight activity. The city’s Department of Aviation reviews annual usage data and can reassign gates when an airline’s utilization changes, creating a direct link between the number of flights an airline operates and the amount of gate real estate it retains.

United’s Recent Gains

According to the Simple Flying report, United Airlines secured additional gates in late 2025 after expanding its flight schedule at O’Hare. The airline’s increased departure volume allowed it to claim more preferential‑use gates under the city’s allocation formula, strengthening its foothold in terminals such as Terminal 3. United’s strategy focused on maximizing turn‑around efficiency and preserving scheduling flexibility at the congested hub.

American’s Counter‑Move

American Airlines responded by filing lobbying efforts and pursuing legal avenues to contest United’s gains. The carrier has also accelerated its own schedule growth, aiming to reclaim gate positions it previously held. Simple Flying notes that American’s push includes targeting the same concourses where United expanded, notably the shared Concourse G area, and seeking a re‑evaluation of the city’s gate‑redistribution decisions.

Broader Implications

The rivalry is not limited to gate counts; it also touches on FAA‑imposed daily flight caps intended to prevent runway saturation. Both airlines are navigating these caps while trying to expand their market share. Travelers could experience changes in flight availability, gate locations, and possibly longer connection times if the dispute leads to further schedule adjustments or court‑ordered reallocations. Industry observers see the O’Hare showdown as a test case for how legacy carriers will compete for resources at other congested airports that use similar gate‑allocation mechanisms.

Key facts

  • O’Hare’s gate assignments are tied to airlines’ departure volumes and reviewed annually by the City of Chicago’s Department of Aviation.
  • United added preferential gates in late 2025 after expanding its flight schedule at the hub.
  • American has launched lobbying and legal actions to challenge United’s recent gate gains.
  • Both carriers must operate within FAA daily flight limits that aim to curb runway congestion.

What this means

The Simple Flying account suggests that the gate‑allocation system at O’Hare incentivizes airlines to increase flight frequencies, turning gate space into a competitive commodity. United’s earlier schedule expansion gave it a quantitative edge, prompting American to adopt a defensive posture that includes legal and political tactics. If the dispute escalates, the city may need to refine its allocation criteria to balance airline growth with passenger convenience, especially as federal caps constrain overall traffic. The situation illustrates how airport governance structures can amplify carrier rivalries, potentially affecting service quality for travelers.
