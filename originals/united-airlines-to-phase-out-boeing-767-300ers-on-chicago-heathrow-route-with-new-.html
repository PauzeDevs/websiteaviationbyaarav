United Airlines to Phase Out Boeing 767‑300ERs on Chicago‑Heathrow Route with New 787‑9 “Elevated” Dreamliners

United plans to replace its aging 767‑300ER business‑shuttle fleet on the Chicago O’Hare‑London Heathrow corridor with a 222‑seat, high‑density Boeing 787‑9 configuration, targeting full 767 retirement by 2030.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-26 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

United Airlines will retire its remaining Boeing 767‑300ERs on the Chicago‑London route, substituting them with a newly configured Boeing 787‑9 dubbed “Elevated.” The new aircraft will feature a larger premium cabin, including Polaris suites and Premium Plus seats, and will join the fleet from San Francisco and Houston, with Houston service slated for January 5 2027. United’s broader Dreamliner order of over 140 aircraft underpins a plan to retire all 53 Boeing 767s by around 2030.

Current 767‑300ER Operations

According to Simple Flying, United continues to operate three daily round‑trip flights between Chicago O’Hare and London Heathrow using Boeing 767‑300ERs that have been in service for nearly three decades. Of the 53 767s still in United’s fleet, 24 are dedicated to high‑yield business‑shuttle service, configured with 167 seats that include 46 lie‑flat Polaris suites and a limited number of premium recliners. This configuration is tighter than typical 767 layouts, which often exceed 200 seats, reflecting United’s focus on premium revenue on this transatlantic corridor.

The “Elevated” 787‑9 Solution

United’s replacement strategy, as reported by Simple Flying, involves deploying a 222‑seat Boeing 787‑9 variant called “Elevated.” The aircraft will allocate roughly half of its cabin to premium products, offering 64 Polaris suites and 35 Premium Plus recliners, while the remainder will be economy seating. The new Dreamliners are powered by GE Aerospace GEnx‑1B engines, consistent with United’s existing 787 fleet. The airline already operates Elevated 787‑9 flights to Heathrow from its San Francisco hub, and plans to introduce the same configuration from Houston on 5 January 2027. United’s broader fleet plan includes more than 140 firm Dreamliner orders, which are intended to replace the aging 767 fleet by around 2030.

Operational and Network Implications

The shift to the 787‑9 is driven more by aircraft delivery schedules than by hub readiness, according to the source. Chicago O’Hare already handles about 20 % of United’s Dreamliner traffic to European destinations such as Italy, Germany, and France, suggesting the airport is equipped to accommodate the new aircraft without major infrastructure changes. The higher seat count and modern cabin amenities are expected to improve passenger experience while maintaining United’s premium capacity on a slot‑constrained Heathrow arrival schedule, especially during winter headwinds.

Future Outlook

United’s plan to retire all remaining 767‑300ERs by roughly 2030 aligns with industry trends favoring fuel‑efficient, composite‑airframe aircraft. The introduction of the Elevated 787‑9 on the Chicago‑Heathrow route is positioned as a flagship upgrade, potentially setting a template for other long‑haul corridors. The airline’s extensive Dreamliner order provides flexibility to reallocate aircraft across hubs as demand evolves, though the exact timing of full 767 phase‑out will depend on delivery cadence and regulatory approvals.

Key facts

  • United operates three daily Chicago‑Heathrow round‑trips with Boeing 767‑300ERs configured for 167‑seat business shuttles
  • The new 222‑seat Boeing 787‑9 “Elevated” will carry 64 Polaris suites and 35 Premium Plus seats
  • Houston service with the Elevated 787‑9 begins 5 January 2027; San Francisco service already active
  • United has ordered over 140 Boeing Dreamliners to retire all 53 remaining 767s by around 2030
  • O’Hare accounts for roughly one‑fifth of United’s Dreamliner flights to European hubs

What this means

The transition from the 767‑300ER to a premium‑focused 787‑9 reflects United’s dual objectives of modernising its fleet and preserving high‑margin premium capacity on a key transatlantic route. By concentrating the new Dreamliners in hubs where the airline already operates a substantial 787 base, United can leverage existing maintenance and crew infrastructure, reducing operational disruption. The larger cabin and newer technology should also deliver fuel‑efficiency gains, supporting United’s broader sustainability targets while offering a more competitive product against rivals on the Chicago‑London market.
