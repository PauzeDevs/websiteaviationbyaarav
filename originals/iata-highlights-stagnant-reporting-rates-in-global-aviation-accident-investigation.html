IATA Highlights Stagnant Reporting Rates in Global Aviation Accident Investigations

Nearly half of all aviation accident investigations remain incomplete, raising concerns about the industry's ability to implement critical safety improvements.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-15 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

The International Air Transport Association (IATA) reports that approximately 50% of aviation accident investigations fail to produce final reports, a trend that experts argue impedes the industry's capacity to learn from past incidents and enhance future safety protocols.

The Reporting Gap

According to data cited by Simple Flying, the International Air Transport Association (IATA) has identified a significant shortfall in the completion of aviation accident investigations. During the International Aviation Safety Summit held in Montreal on November 12, 2026, IATA safety director Mark Searle characterized the current publication rate—which hovers near 50%—as unacceptable. A joint position paper released in February 2025 revealed that of 268 accidents recorded by IATA between 2018 and 2023, 128 cases remained without a final report as of mid-2024.

Regulatory Framework and Objectives

The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) provides the governing framework for these investigations through Annex 13 to the Convention on International Civil Aviation. The established guidelines mandate the release of a preliminary report within 30 days, with the expectation that a final report be published within 12 months. If an investigation extends beyond this one-year window, authorities are expected to issue annual interim statements. The primary objective of these investigations is to identify safety hazards and issue recommendations for manufacturers, regulators, and airlines, rather than to assign legal liability.

High-Profile Cases and Safety Implications

The absence of final reports has drawn attention to several major incidents. Simple Flying reports that the June 2025 crash of an Air India Boeing 787 in Ahmedabad, which resulted in 260 fatalities, and the March 2022 crash of China Eastern Airlines Flight MU5735, which claimed 132 lives, have yet to produce publicly available final reports. While interim updates provide some data, IATA maintains that they are insufficient substitutes for comprehensive analyses that detail the root causes of accidents and provide actionable safety recommendations.

Key facts

  • IATA reports that 48% of accidents recorded between 2018 and 2023 lacked final investigation reports as of June 2024.
  • ICAO Annex 13 guidelines recommend that final accident reports be published within 12 months of an incident.
  • The June 2025 Air India Boeing 787 crash and the March 2022 China Eastern Flight 5735 crash remain without final reports.
  • IATA emphasizes that the primary goal of accident investigation is safety prevention rather than the determination of legal blame.

What this means

The persistent delay in finalizing accident reports suggests a systemic failure to adhere to ICAO's one-year target. By failing to close investigations, the aviation industry loses critical opportunities to disseminate safety lessons, potentially leaving similar hazards unaddressed in the global fleet. The reliance on interim reports, while helpful for immediate transparency, does not provide the depth required for long-term systemic safety improvements.
