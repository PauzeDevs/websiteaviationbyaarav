AutoFlight Europe unveils the V5000 Matrix hybrid‑electric eVTOL

The 10‑seat V5000 Matrix, a lift‑plus‑cruise design with a lavatory and regional focus, has begun flight testing and is targeting CAAC certification, according to Aviation Week.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-22 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Aviation Week reports that AutoFlight Europe GmbH’s V5000 Matrix, a lift‑plus‑cruise hybrid‑electric/e‑electric eVTOL, was unveiled in February 2026 and has already demonstrated a remotely piloted transition from hover to cruise. The 10‑seat aircraft, with a maximum take‑off weight of 5,700 kg, can be configured for VIP or business use and will be the first eVTOL to include a lavatory. Certification is planned with China’s CAAC under powered‑lift rules, and industry observers speculate service entry could occur in 2027‑2028, though no official timeline has been confirmed.

Program launch and flight‑test milestone

Aviation Week notes that AutoFlight Europe GmbH introduced the V5000 Matrix in February 2026. Within weeks, the company conducted a remotely piloted flight that transitioned from a thrust‑borne hover to wing‑borne cruise, confirming that flight‑test activity is already underway. The report does not provide performance data such as range, speed or endurance, but the successful transition demonstrates the viability of the lift‑plus‑cruise architecture for a hybrid‑electric eVTOL.

Design configuration and passenger amenities

The Matrix is described as a 10‑seat eVTOL with a hybrid‑electric powertrain and a maximum take‑off weight of 5,700 kg. Two cabin layouts are outlined: a six‑seat VIP arrangement and a nine‑seat business configuration that includes a pilot. Notably, the aircraft will feature a lavatory—the first eVTOL design to do so—signalling an intention for longer regional missions. An uncrewed cargo variant is also under development. Structurally, the V5000 evolves from AutoFlight’s earlier Prosperity I and CarryAll models, incorporating a long aerodynamic cabin with panoramic windows, skid‑style landing gear and a high‑mounted wing.

Certification pathway and market positioning

According to the source, AutoFlight plans to seek certification from the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC) under the agency’s powered‑lift regulations. The company is targeting a regional market, positioning the Matrix as a longer‑range alternative to earlier eVTOL concepts that focus on short‑haul urban trips. Industry commentators cited in the article have speculated that entry into service could occur as early as 2027‑2028, but the report emphasizes that no formal timetable has been confirmed by the manufacturer.

Industry context and outlook

The V5000 Matrix reflects a broader trend toward hybrid‑electric eVTOLs that aim to bridge the gap between pure electric short‑range vehicles and conventional turboprop aircraft. By incorporating a lavatory and a higher passenger capacity, AutoFlight appears to be addressing operator demand for regional connectivity rather than solely urban air mobility. The pursuit of CAAC certification also suggests a strategic focus on the Chinese market, where regulatory frameworks for powered‑lift aircraft are being actively developed.

What this means

The V5000 Matrix represents a notable shift in eVTOL design philosophy, moving from ultra‑short urban hops toward longer regional routes. By integrating a lavatory and higher passenger capacity, AutoFlight is addressing operational requirements that have limited earlier pure‑electric concepts. The hybrid‑electric architecture, combined with a lift‑plus‑cruise configuration, may offer a pragmatic path to meeting performance and range expectations while staying within emerging powered‑lift certification frameworks. If CAAC certification proceeds smoothly, the aircraft could become one of the first eVTOLs to serve inter‑city corridors, potentially influencing market expectations and regulatory approaches worldwide.
