Diamond Aircraft Unveils Lift‑and‑Cruise eVTOL V.MO

Aviation Week reports that Diamond Aircraft Industries, backed by Wanfeng Auto, is developing the V.MO, a five‑seat electric vertical‑take‑off and landing aircraft featuring distributed propulsion and a twin‑boom layout.

At a glance

The V.MO eVTOL, a lift‑and‑cruise concept from Diamond Aircraft Industries GmbH, combines a pilot‑plus‑four passenger cabin with ten electric motors driving eight lift fans and two pusher propellers. Originating from a Volkswagen China design transferred in early 2024, the aircraft’s subscale prototype flew in 2022 and the full‑scale model was displayed at the Zhuhai Airshow in November 2024. No certification timetable has been disclosed, and battery specifications remain undefined.

Program Overview

Aviation Week describes the V.MO as a lift‑and‑cruise eVTOL being pursued by Diamond Aircraft Industries GmbH, a subsidiary of the Wanfeng Auto Holding Group. The program targets a five‑seat configuration—one pilot and four passengers—within a streamlined cabin. The aircraft’s maximum take‑off weight is listed between 2,000 and 2,500 kg (4,400–5,500 lb), with a projected range of 200 km (125 mi) and a cruising speed of 200 kph (125 mph). The report notes that, as of its publication date, Diamond has not announced any formal certification roadmap.

Design and Propulsion Architecture

According to the source, the V.MO adopts a twin‑boom layout linked by fore and aft wings. Distributed electric propulsion (DEP) is central to the design: ten electric motors each drive an individual propeller, comprising eight vertically oriented lift fans mounted along the booms and two rear‑mounted pusher propellers for forward thrust during wing‑borne cruise. This arrangement is intended to provide high redundancy, eliminating single points of failure. The aircraft’s flight‑control system is described only in fragmentary terms, with the source cutting off before full details are provided. Battery capacity and chemistry are not disclosed.

Development Timeline

The Aviation Week excerpt traces the V.MO’s lineage to a design originally created by Volkswagen China. A transfer agreement in February 2024 moved the concept to Diamond Aircraft, which subsequently built a subscale prototype that achieved its first flight in 2022. The full‑scale version was publicly unveiled at the Zhuhai Airshow in November 2024, where the manufacturer showcased the aircraft’s external geometry and propulsion layout. No further milestones, such as flight‑test campaigns or type‑certification submissions, are mentioned in the source.

Market Outlook and Certification Outlook

While the source does not provide explicit market forecasts, the V.MO’s specifications place it within the emerging segment of lift‑and‑cruise eVTOLs aimed at short‑range regional transport. The reported range of 200 km and passenger capacity align with anticipated use cases such as air‑taxi services and point‑to‑point connectivity in congested corridors. However, the absence of announced certification plans and the lack of battery data introduce uncertainty regarding the program’s timeline to commercial operation. Industry observers typically view certification pathways as a critical hurdle for eVTOL adoption, and the V.MO’s progress will likely depend on regulatory engagement and demonstrable safety performance.

What this means

The V.MO reflects a growing trend among manufacturers to leverage distributed electric propulsion for redundancy and safety. By integrating lift and cruise capabilities in a single airframe, Diamond aims to address both vertical take‑off constraints and efficient forward flight, a combination that could broaden operational flexibility if certification hurdles are cleared. The partnership origin—transfer from Volkswagen China—suggests cross‑industry collaboration, potentially accelerating technology transfer but also adding complexity to program management. The lack of disclosed battery details and certification intent highlights the early stage of the effort; without these, the aircraft’s commercial viability remains speculative.
