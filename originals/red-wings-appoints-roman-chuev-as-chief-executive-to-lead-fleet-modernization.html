Red Wings Appoints Roman Chuev as Chief Executive to Lead Fleet Modernization

The Russian carrier transitions leadership to a former airport group executive as it prepares for a transition to domestic-built aircraft.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Red Wings has named Roman Chuev, formerly of Novaport Holding, as its new CEO. Chuev is tasked with overseeing the airline's fleet modernization strategy, which centers on the integration of new Russian-manufactured aircraft.

Leadership Transition at Red Wings

Russian airline Red Wings has officially appointed Roman Chuev to the position of chief executive officer. Chuev joins the carrier from his previous role as the head of Novaport Holding, an airport group. His appointment follows the departure of former chief executive Alexander Lebedev. According to reports from FlightGlobal, the airline was briefly managed by commercial director Igor Ivliev in the interim period between Lebedev's exit and Chuev's arrival.

Strategic Focus on Fleet Modernization

The primary mandate for the incoming chief executive is the modernization of the Red Wings fleet. The airline, which currently operates a core fleet consisting of Superjet 100 aircraft, is preparing to integrate new Russian-built models into its operations. This shift is part of a broader strategic effort to update the carrier's capacity and operational capabilities.

Context of the Appointment

The leadership change comes at a pivotal time for the carrier as it navigates the complexities of fleet renewal. While the airline has confirmed its intent to transition toward domestic aircraft, the specific timeline and the exact models to be introduced remain part of the broader modernization plan that Chuev will now oversee. These claims regarding the airline's strategic direction and leadership changes were reported by FlightGlobal and have not been independently corroborated by other sources.

Key facts

  • Roman Chuev has been appointed as the new CEO of Red Wings.
  • Chuev previously served as the head of the airport group Novaport Holding.
  • The airline is currently focused on a fleet modernization program involving new Russian-built aircraft.
  • The carrier's existing core fleet is comprised of Superjet 100 aircraft.

What this means

The appointment of an executive with a background in airport management suggests that Red Wings may be prioritizing operational efficiency and infrastructure integration as it prepares to introduce new aircraft types. The shift toward Russian-built models aligns with broader industry trends in the region regarding fleet self-sufficiency.
