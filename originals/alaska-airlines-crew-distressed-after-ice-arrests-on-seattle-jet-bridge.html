Alaska Airlines Crew Distressed After ICE Arrests on Seattle Jet Bridge

Simple Flying reports that a pilot walked off and two flight attendants asked for replacement during boarding of Alaska flight AS1306 after ICE detained two passengers, causing a three‑hour delay.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-22 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to Simple Flying, US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) arrested two passengers on a Seattle‑Tacoma International Airport jet bridge while Alaska Airlines flight AS1306 to Los Angeles was boarding. Video shows officers confronting travelers, and a passenger recounts that a pilot left the aircraft and two flight attendants requested to be substituted. Alaska Airlines said it was not given advance notice of the enforcement action and is reviewing the incident. The flight was delayed more than three hours. The account has not been independently corroborated.

Incident Overview

Simple Flying states that on the morning of 22 November 2026, ICE agents moved onto gate N9 at Seattle‑Tacoma International Airport to detain two individuals—identified by ICE as a Mexican national accused of overstaying a visa and a second passenger alleged to have obstructed officers. Video footage released by the outlet shows masked agents grappling with the travelers while other passengers were pressed against the jet bridge. The boarding process for Alaska Airlines flight AS1306, a Boeing 737‑800 bound for Los Angeles, was halted, and the flight ultimately departed after a delay exceeding three hours.

Crew Response

A passenger interviewed by local radio station KUOW, identified as Aidan Mendoza, described seeing an Alaska pilot retreat up the jet bridge appearing uncomfortable and not returning to the aircraft. The same interview reported that two Alaska flight attendants asked to be "subbed out" because they felt overwhelmed by the situation. Mendoza chose not to board the flight after a gate agent removed his name from the passenger list; he later received a ticket refund.

Airline and ICE Statements

Simple Flying notes that an Alaska Airlines spokesperson declined to confirm crew replacements but said the airline had not received advance notice of the ICE operation and was working to understand the circumstances. The carrier expressed concern for employees and guests. ICE, in a statement cited by Simple Flying, said the operation targeted Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, unrelated to the passenger Aidan Mendoza, and claimed that other travelers impeded the arrests, with one passenger allegedly throwing hot coffee at officers.

Operational Impact

The boarding disruption resulted in a delay of more than three hours for flight AS1306, affecting approximately 150 passengers. Simple Flying reports that the incident sparked widespread outrage on social media, raising questions about the coordination between law‑enforcement actions and airline operations. No further flights were reported to have been affected, and the airline indicated it was reviewing its protocols for handling similar situations in the future.

What this means

The incident highlights the operational vulnerability of airlines when law‑enforcement actions intersect with boarding procedures. Crew members reported heightened stress, which can translate into safety and service concerns if not promptly addressed. Alaska Airlines' lack of prior notification suggests a gap in communication protocols between agencies and carriers, potentially prompting airlines to seek clearer guidelines to protect crew welfare and maintain schedule integrity. The public reaction captured by Simple Flying indicates reputational risk for both the airline and ICE when enforcement actions appear to disrupt passenger safety and comfort.
