EHang Advances VT35 eVTOL Toward Flight Testing Phase

Aviation Week reports that the Chinese maker’s two‑seat lift‑plus‑cruise electric vertical take‑off and landing aircraft has completed assembly and is slated for its inaugural public flight in December 2025, with certification pursued through the CAAC.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-22 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Aviation Week details the progress of EHang’s VT35, an autonomous lift‑plus‑cruise eVTOL designed for two occupants. The prototype has finished final assembly and entered flight‑test campaigns, with a public demonstration planned for December 2025. Certification will be sought from China’s civil aviation authority, leveraging experience from the earlier EH216‑S model. The aircraft employs distributed electric propulsion across eight lift fans and a rear pusher for cruise, promising redundancy and safety, though many performance parameters remain undisclosed.

Program Milestones

According to Aviation Week, the VT35 prototype has moved beyond the build stage and is now in the flight‑testing arena. The company aims to showcase the aircraft publicly in December 2025. Certification efforts will be directed to the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC), mirroring the pathway taken for the EH216‑S, and EHang expects an accelerated schedule based on its prior certification experience.

Design Architecture and Propulsion

The VT35 is described as a lift‑plus‑cruise configuration that seats two passengers within a cabin featuring wide‑angle windows. Its lift system consists of eight electrically driven fans mounted on tandem wings—four oriented upward and four downward—to provide vertical lift. A separate rear‑mounted pusher propeller supplies forward thrust for cruise flight. Each fan operates with its own motor, a layout termed distributed electric propulsion (DEP). The manufacturer claims this arrangement eliminates single points of failure, thereby enhancing onboard safety. Specifics such as cruising altitude, speed, and endurance have not been disclosed.

Physical Characteristics and Performance Estimates

Aviation Week lists the VT35’s maximum take‑off weight at roughly 2,094 pounds (950 kilograms) and a payload capacity of about 440 pounds (200 kilograms). The airframe measures approximately 26 feet (8 meters) in both length and wingspan. While the landing gear configuration is not confirmed, earlier versions employed a fixed tricycle arrangement. The aircraft’s advertised range is 124 miles (200 kilometers), but other performance metrics remain unspecified.

Market Implications and Outlook

If the VT35 achieves certification on the projected timeline, it could join a growing segment of autonomous, short‑range eVTOLs targeting urban air mobility and private travel markets. The two‑seat capacity positions it for niche applications such as executive transport or air‑taxi services on low‑density routes. However, the lack of detailed speed, altitude, and endurance data makes it difficult to assess competitiveness against other emerging platforms.
