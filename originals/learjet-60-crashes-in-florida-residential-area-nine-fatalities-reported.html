Learjet 60 Crashes in Florida Residential Area; Nine Fatalities Reported

A business jet traveling from Texas to the Bahamas impacted near Greenacres, Florida, prompting local evacuations and an ongoing investigation.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

A Bombardier Learjet 60, registered as N722BD, crashed in Greenacres, Florida, on November 1, 2026, resulting in the deaths of all nine people on board. The aircraft was en route from Austin, Texas, to the Bahamas when it experienced a rapid descent.

Incident Overview

On Sunday, November 1, 2026, a Bombardier Learjet 60 (serial number 60-271) crashed in a residential area of Greenacres, Florida, near the intersection of Military Trail and 10th Avenue North. The location is approximately six miles from the Mar-a-Lago estate. According to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), nine individuals were on board the aircraft, all of whom were killed in the incident. The jet, which had departed from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS) and was destined for North Eleuthera Airport (ELH) in the Bahamas, erupted into a fire upon impact.

Flight Data and ATC Reports

Simple Flying reports that the aircraft reached a cruising altitude of 41,000 feet before entering a sudden, high-speed descent over the West Palm Beach area. Citing online air traffic control (ATC) audio, the report states that the pilots had communicated concerns regarding icing and moderate turbulence shortly before the aircraft began a descent rate of 20,000 feet per minute. These claims regarding the ATC communications have not been independently corroborated by official investigative bodies.

Local Impact and Aftermath

The crash caused significant damage to local infrastructure, including power lines and water mains. Local authorities initiated the evacuation of approximately 600 residents from a nearby trailer park to emergency shelters. Additionally, a local school was closed on Monday, November 2, 2026, due to the disruption. Officials have indicated that the road impacted by the crash may remain closed for at least a month while recovery and investigative efforts continue.

Key facts

  • The aircraft involved was a Bombardier Learjet 60, registration N722BD.
  • Nine fatalities were confirmed by the FAA following the crash in Greenacres, Florida.
  • The flight originated in Austin, Texas, and was intended for the Bahamas.

What this means

The incident highlights the catastrophic potential of high-altitude loss-of-control events. While the reported pilot communications regarding icing and turbulence provide a potential environmental context, the extreme descent rate of 20,000 feet per minute suggests a severe mechanical or aerodynamic failure that investigators will need to reconcile with the flight-tracking data.
