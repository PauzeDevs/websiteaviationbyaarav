Ground Stop Context and Scope

On the afternoon of 17 November 2026, the Federal Aviation Administration imposed a nationwide ground stop that halted departures across the United States. Simple Flying describes the event as the longest such stoppage since the September 2001 attacks. While the FAA’s directive applied to all carriers, United Airlines experienced a particularly acute impact at Chicago O’Hare International Airport, where several flights remained on the tarmac for many hours while awaiting clearance to depart.

Passenger Accounts of Extended Delays

Simple Flying relays two passenger testimonies that illustrate the length of the delay. Abdul Sankoh was scheduled to fly United Flight — ORD to IAD at 2:21 p.m. on Friday. According to the report, the aircraft never left the gate and Sankoh was not permitted to disembark until close to 9:00 p.m., amounting to roughly seven hours on the tarmac. He returned to O’Hare the following day to rebook and spent an additional four hours in line. A second passenger, Bryson Moser, was on a United flight from ORD to Charleston, South Carolina. His aircraft endured an eight‑hour ground delay before the flight was ultimately cancelled and passengers were allowed to exit the plane. Moser’s comments, as quoted in the article, note that the pilot attempted to provide updates but that United Airlines did not respond to his calls for further information.

Regulatory Framework and Exceptions

The article references U.S. Department of Transportation regulations that generally require airlines to allow passengers to deplane after three hours of a domestic tarmac delay, or four hours for international flights. Simple Flying points out that exceptions exist, permitting longer confinement under certain circumstances, though it does not specify the criteria applied in this instance. The report also notes that United Airlines has not yet issued a statement regarding the incidents, and Simple Flying’s outreach to the carrier remains unanswered.

Implications for Airline Operations and Passenger Rights

If the extended confinement fell under an allowable exception, United would be in compliance with the letter of the regulation, but the passenger accounts raise concerns about transparency and communication during prolonged delays. The lack of a public response from United limits the ability to assess whether the airline provided adequate accommodations, such as food, water, or restroom access, as required by the rules. The incidents also highlight the operational strain that a nationwide ground stop can place on hub airports, where multiple carriers must manage aircraft, crew, and passenger flow under a single regulatory ceiling.
