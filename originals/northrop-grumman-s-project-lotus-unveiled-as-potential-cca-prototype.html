Northrop Grumman’s Project Lotus Unveiled as Potential CCA Prototype

A previously hidden uncrewed aircraft system spotted at Scaled Composites may be Northrop’s entry for the Air Force’s Collaborative Combat Aircraft Increment 2 program, according to Aviation Week.

At a glance

Aviation Week reports that Northrop Grumman has assembled a large, turbofan‑powered unmanned aircraft, designated Project Lotus, at its Scaled Composites facility in Mojave, California. The aircraft’s configuration differs from Lockheed Martin’s Project Vectis, featuring a high‑mounted engine inlet and sharply canted tail surfaces. The source suggests Lotus is intended for the U.S. Air Force’s Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) Increment 2 competition, with prototype selection slated for next year. No other outlets have confirmed the details.

Background and Discovery

According to a source cited by Aviation Week, Northrop Grumman has been developing a new uncrewed aircraft system under the internal name Project Lotus. The aircraft was observed parked at the public airport adjacent to the Scaled Composites rapid‑prototyping site in Mojave, California. The observation was based on a photograph reviewed by the publication, which shows a completed airframe with a turbofan engine installed.

Design Characteristics

The Lotus design is described as having a long, slender fuselage positioned forward of the wing leading edges, ending in a pointed nose with swept‑back edges. Unlike the low‑mounted engine inlet of Lockheed Martin’s Project Vectis, Lotus places its inlet high on the aft fuselage. Additionally, the aircraft features sharply canted tail surfaces, contrasting with the tailless layout of Vectis. These visual cues suggest a distinct aerodynamic approach, though exact performance metrics remain undisclosed.

Potential Role in the CCA Program

Aviation Week links the Lotus demonstrator to the Air Force’s Collaborative Combat Aircraft Increment 2 (CCA‑2) effort, which is finalising requirements for prototype submissions ahead of a planned acquisition timeline next year. If confirmed, Lotus would represent Northrop Grumman’s candidate for the competition, which seeks a high‑speed, long‑range, autonomous platform capable of operating alongside manned fighters. The article notes that the demonstrator appears to be ready for testing, but no official statements from Northrop or the Air Force have been reported.
