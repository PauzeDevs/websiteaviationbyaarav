Ryanair Mulls Temporary Pay Freeze as Fuel Costs Set to Surge by €2 Billion

The Irish low‑cost carrier is reviewing labour costs after projecting a €2 billion rise in its 2028 fuel bill, driven by expiring hedges and geopolitical volatility, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Ryanair has signalled that a temporary freeze on pay increases may be required to offset a projected €2 billion increase in its 2028 fuel expenses. The airline attributes the cost pressure to the expiry of fuel hedges, ongoing conflict in the Middle East and Ukraine, and broader macro‑economic shocks. While higher fares and a rise in passenger traffic are expected in 2027, Simple Flying reports that these factors alone are unlikely to fully cover the anticipated fuel surge. The carrier stresses that a pay freeze would not mean a loss of salary, only a suspension of inflation‑linked raises, and notes that the proposal remains under review.

Rising Fuel Costs and Hedging Expiry

Simple Flying notes that Ryanair’s fuel bill for the 2028 financial year is expected to climb by roughly €2 billion. The airline attributes the jump to the imminent expiry of its fuel‑hedge contracts, which currently shield about 20 % of its jet fuel consumption. With the hedged portion set to lapse, the carrier will be exposed to spot‑market prices that have been buoyed by recent geopolitical tensions.

Geopolitical and Operational Headwinds

According to the source, Ryanair cites several external risk factors that could further strain profitability. These include the escalation of conflict in the Middle East and Ukraine, macro‑economic shocks, and disruptions to European air‑traffic control (ATC) services. The airline’s own outlook for 2027 flags these variables as potential dampeners on full‑year profit, even as passenger traffic is projected to rise.

Potential Pay Freeze as a Cost‑Containment Measure

In response to the projected fuel cost surge, Ryanair is reportedly reviewing all cost categories, with particular focus on maturing labour agreements. Simple Flying reports that the airline is considering a temporary freeze on pay increases, meaning that while salaries would continue to be paid, any inflation‑linked raises would be suspended for the duration of the measure. The carrier emphasizes that the proposal is still under review and is one of several cost‑saving options being explored.

Outlook and Uncertainties

The airline expects an increase in traffic for the 2027 financial year, which could provide some offset to higher operating costs. However, Simple Flying stresses that higher fares alone are unlikely to fully compensate for the anticipated €2 billion fuel bill rise. The pay‑freeze proposal, if adopted, would be a short‑term response pending a longer‑term solution to fuel price volatility.

Key facts

  • Ryanair projects a €2 billion increase in its 2028 fuel bill as hedged fuel contracts expire
  • The carrier cites Middle‑East and Ukraine conflicts, macro‑economic shocks, and European ATC disruptions as profit‑impacting factors
  • A temporary pay‑freeze, not a salary cut, is being reviewed to contain costs
  • Higher fares and expected traffic growth in 2027 are deemed insufficient to fully offset the fuel cost surge

What this means

Based on the information supplied by Simple Flying, Ryanair’s consideration of a pay freeze reflects a strategic effort to manage a sharp rise in fuel expenses that it cannot fully hedge against. The airline’s exposure to spot‑market fuel prices, combined with external geopolitical and operational risks, creates a fiscal environment where traditional revenue levers—such as fare hikes and traffic growth—may not be enough. By targeting labour cost growth, Ryanair aims to preserve cash flow while it seeks longer‑term solutions, such as renewed hedging strategies or operational efficiencies. The approach underscores the broader vulnerability of low‑cost carriers to fuel price volatility, especially when hedging windows close.
