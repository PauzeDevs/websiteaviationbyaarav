ITA Airways eyes Milan‑Lima link to pre‑empt Emirates’ fifth‑freedom bid

ITA Airways is reportedly planning to restart long‑haul flights from Milan Malpensa to Lima, using a rotating Airbus A350 service that would also connect Rome, in an effort to block Emirates’ proposed fifth‑freedom operation on the same city‑pair.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-04 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to Simple Flying, the Italian flag carrier intends to re‑establish a Milan‑Lima route from Malpensa Airport, employing a ‘W‑shaped’ rotation with Rome and leveraging an Airbus A350. The move is framed as a defensive strategy against Emirates, which is said to be exploring a fifth‑freedom service from Milan to Lima.

Background and recent long‑haul activity

Simple Flying notes that ITA Airways has been absent from scheduled long‑haul operations out of Milan Malpensa for almost three years. The carrier’s last such flight from the airport was a New York service in January 2024. Since then, ITA has confined its Milan operations to Linate Airport, focusing on short‑haul and European routes.

Proposed Milan‑Lima service and operational concept

The report details a plan to launch a Milan‑Lima connection that would not require a permanent base at Malpensa. Instead, ITA would operate a rotating schedule that starts in Rome, flies to Lima, proceeds to Milan, returns to Lima, and finally circles back to Rome. This ‘W‑shaped’ rotation would be serviced by an Airbus A350 and is expected to run two to three times weekly, linking both Italian capitals with the Peruvian capital.

Competitive context with Emirates

Simple Flying cites Corriere della Sera as the source for Emirates’ interest in a fifth‑freedom Milan‑Lima flight, extending its existing Dubai‑Milan‑New York network. Emirates already operates a Milan‑New York fifth‑freedom service, and the proposed Lima extension would give the UAE carrier a direct presence in South America from Italy. ITA’s announced intent is framed as a pre‑emptive move to block that expansion, with the airline’s chief executive Jörg Eberhart emphasizing the carrier’s duty to serve regional needs and bolster Italy’s competitiveness.

Strategic implications for ITA and the Italian market

If realised, the Milan‑Lima route would restore ITA’s long‑haul footprint at Malpensa and diversify its network beyond the Europe‑centric schedule that has dominated since the airline’s 2021 formation. The use of a single A350 on a rotating circuit could optimise aircraft utilisation while limiting the need for extensive ground infrastructure at Malpensa. Moreover, by offering a direct Italy‑Peru link, ITA could capture demand from business travelers, diaspora communities, and tourism, potentially offsetting any market share Emirates might seek to gain through its fifth‑freedom ambitions.

What this means

The proposed Milan‑Lima service appears to be a strategic response to a perceived competitive threat rather than a purely demand‑driven expansion. By re‑entering Malpensa with a flexible rotating schedule, ITA can signal market presence without the capital outlay of a full long‑haul base. The move also aligns with the carrier’s broader goal of strengthening Italy’s global connectivity, a narrative reinforced by the CEO’s public comments. However, the success of the plan will hinge on securing traffic rights, slot availability at Malpensa and Lima, and the commercial viability of a twice‑weekly A350 operation, factors not addressed in the source material.
