Malaysia Airlines Orders Boeing 787‑10 Dreamliners to Phase Out A350 Fleet

The carrier has signed a deal for up to 16 stretched Dreamliners, with deliveries slated for the early‑to‑mid‑2030s, marking Malaysia’s first Boeing 787 operation and a major step in its fleet renewal plan.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-25 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Malaysia Airlines has committed to acquire up to 16 Boeing 787‑10 aircraft – 10 firm orders and six options – to replace its seven Airbus A350‑900 jets. Deliveries are expected between 2032 and 2035. The move, reported by Simple Flying, will make the carrier the first Malaysian airline to operate the Dreamliner and reflects a strategic shift toward higher‑capacity, fuel‑efficient widebodies powered by GE GEnx‑1B engines.

Deal Overview and Timeline

According to Simple Flying, Malaysia Airlines signed a memorandum with Boeing in Kuala Lumpur that secures up to 16 787‑10 Dreamliners. The agreement comprises ten firm orders and six optional units, with the first aircraft slated for delivery in 2032 and the final unit expected by 2035. The signing ceremony was attended by representatives from Boeing, GE Aerospace and Malaysian officials, underscoring the bilateral nature of the partnership.

Fleet Replacement Strategy

The airline plans to retire its fleet of seven Airbus A350‑900 aircraft, which, per ch‑aviation data cited by Simple Flying, average 8.5 years of service. The retirement window aligns with the 787‑10 delivery schedule, allowing a seamless transition. Simple Flying notes that the carrier intends to configure each Dreamliner with 319 seats across three classes, a capacity that sits between the 787‑9’s 250‑325 seats and the 787‑8’s 200‑275 seats, according to Boeing’s published data.

Technical Choices and Market Impact

Malaysia Airlines has selected the GE Aerospace GEnx‑1B turbofan to power its 787‑10 fleet, a detail confirmed by Simple Flying. The choice reflects a broader industry trend favoring the GEnx for its fuel efficiency and commonality across Boeing’s 787 family. By becoming the first Malaysian operator of the Dreamliner, the airline signals confidence in the aircraft’s long‑range, high‑capacity capabilities, potentially influencing other regional carriers evaluating fleet renewal options.

Strategic Implications

In a statement quoted by Simple Flying, Malaysia Airlines described the order as a "significant step" in its long‑term renewal strategy, emphasizing a commitment to a modern, future‑ready fleet. Boeing’s Southeast Asia President, Travis Sullivan, was also quoted as expressing pride in the partnership and highlighting the role of local suppliers. The move may enhance Malaysia’s aviation ecosystem by fostering deeper ties with Boeing and its supply chain, while also offering passengers a newer product with improved cabin space.

Key facts

  • Up to 16 Boeing 787‑10 Dreamliners (10 firm orders, 6 options)
  • Deliveries scheduled for 2032‑2035
  • Will replace seven Airbus A350‑900 jets averaging 8.5 years old
  • First Malaysian airline to operate the Boeing 787
  • Configured for 319 seats in a three‑class layout, powered by GE GEnx‑1B engines

What this means

The order represents a strategic pivot for Malaysia Airlines, shifting from Airbus’s A350 platform to Boeing’s larger 787‑10 to meet anticipated demand for higher‑capacity routes. Aligning aircraft retirements with new deliveries minimizes fleet gaps and preserves operational continuity. Selecting the GEnx engine aligns with industry preferences for fuel‑efficient powerplants, potentially reducing operating costs over the aircraft’s lifecycle. As the first Malaysian 787 operator, the airline may gain a competitive edge in offering a newer cabin experience, while also deepening its integration with Boeing’s global supply network, which could yield ancillary economic benefits for local aerospace firms.
