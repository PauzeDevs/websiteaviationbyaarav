Southwest Airlines Announces Interline Partnership with French bee to Extend International Reach

Southwest’s new interline agreement with French bee links its domestic network to the French carrier’s long‑haul routes via LAX, MIA and SFO, offering single‑ticket travel to Europe, the Indian Ocean and upcoming Asian destinations.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-17 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Southwest Airlines has entered an interline partnership with French bee, its 12th international airline partner, enabling passengers to book combined itineraries across the two carriers. The agreement, effective immediately, connects Southwest’s domestic hubs to French bee’s Airbus A350 services from Paris‑Orly to destinations including Newark, Los Angeles, Miami, San Francisco, Montreal, Réunion Island and Tahiti. While the partnership expands Southwest’s global footprint without the airline operating its own long‑haul flights, bookings are not yet available on Southwest.com and loyalty benefits are excluded.

Partnership Overview

Simple Flying reports that Southwest Airlines and French bee have formalised an interline partnership, the latest addition to Southwest’s growing list of overseas connections that began with Icelandair in early 2025. The agreement is described as an interline rather than a codeshare, meaning the airlines will coordinate connecting itineraries and baggage handling while retaining separate flight numbers. The partnership is said to be active immediately, with reservations accessible through French bee’s channels, travel agents and third‑party platforms.

Operational Details

According to the source, the interline link will operate through three major U.S. gateways: Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), Miami International Airport (MIA) and San Francisco International Airport (SFO). These hubs will feed into French bee’s long‑haul Airbus A350 fleet, which is based at Paris‑Orly. French bee’s current network includes service to Newark, Los Angeles, Miami, San Francisco, Montreal, Réunion Island and Tahiti, and the carrier is preparing to launch routes to two Asian markets. The article notes that the partnership does not yet allow ticketing via Southwest.com, nor does it include reciprocal frequent‑flyer earning or redemption.

Strategic Implications

Southwest Executive Vice President and Chief Commercial Officer Justin Jones is quoted as emphasizing the strategic value of the deal, highlighting the airline’s desire to broaden international options without operating its own long‑haul aircraft. Simple Flying frames the partnership as a continuation of Southwest’s “remarkable expansion of overseas connectivity,” positioning the carrier to capture demand for trans‑Atlantic and Indian Ocean travel while leveraging French bee’s all‑Airbus A350 fleet. The source also mentions that French bee is owned by Groupe Dubreuil and operates the world’s longest nonstop domestic route between Paris and Réunion Island, underscoring its capability in ultra‑long‑haul operations.

Passenger Experience

For travelers, the interline agreement promises a single ticket for journeys that combine Southwest’s domestic legs with French bee’s international segments, simplifying baggage transfer and itinerary management. However, the source points out two current limitations: the inability to book through Southwest’s website and the exclusion of loyalty program benefits. These constraints may affect the appeal to frequent‑flyer members, though the partnership still expands destination choices for price‑sensitive customers seeking value‑oriented travel across oceans.

Key facts

  • Southwest’s 12th international partner is French bee, an all‑Airbus A350 carrier based at Paris‑Orly.
  • The interline agreement links Southwest’s LAX, MIA and SFO hubs to French bee’s long‑haul network.
  • Bookings are available via French bee, travel agents and third‑party platforms, but not through Southwest.com.
  • Loyalty points cannot be earned or redeemed on the interline tickets at this time.

What this means

The interline arrangement allows Southwest to extend its market reach into Europe, the Indian Ocean and soon Asia without the capital outlay of operating its own wide‑body fleet. By leveraging French bee’s existing A350 capacity, Southwest can offer value‑focused customers a broader product suite while maintaining its low‑cost operating model. The exclusion of Southwest.com ticketing and loyalty integration suggests a phased rollout, possibly to gauge demand before deeper system integration. If passenger uptake proves strong, the partnership could serve as a template for further interline deals, reinforcing Southwest’s strategy of network expansion through collaborative agreements rather than fleet diversification.
