United Airlines Mobilises Three Flights to Return 500 Texas Students After Macy’s Parade Postponement

Following a historic storm‑induced delay of the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade, United Airlines deployed three aircraft from New York to Houston, ferrying the Pearland High School band and color guard home on Saturday night, according to Simple Flying.

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that United Airlines arranged three dedicated flights from LaGuardia and Newark to Houston International on November 28, 2026, to bring home roughly 500 members of the Pearland High School band and color guard after the parade was postponed due to a severe coastal storm. The effort was funded by a $350,000 community fundraiser led by former Pearland mayor Kevin Cole. United’s involvement shifted the return plan from a bus‑only option on Monday/Tuesday to same‑day air travel, though the airline could not be reached for comment at the time of reporting. No independent verification of the details has been found.

Storm Forces First‑Ever Parade Delay

A coastal storm forecast to hit New York City forced the organizers of the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade to postpone the event for the first time in its century‑long history. The parade, originally set for Thursday, November 26, was rescheduled for Saturday, November 28, with a kickoff at 8:30 a.m. EST. The sudden change disrupted travel arrangements for thousands of participants, including the Pride of Pearland Band and Color Guard from Pearland High School, Texas.

United Steps In With Dedicated Aircraft

Simple Flying reports that United Airlines, a Star Alliance carrier, allocated three extra flights to transport the Texas students and their chaperones from New York back to Houston on Saturday evening. Two of the flights were scheduled to depart LaGuardia Airport (LGA) and one from Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR), with arrivals at George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) slated between 9:30 p.m. and 11:00 p.m. local time. The airline’s rapid response replaced an earlier plan that would have relied on bus transportation on Monday or Tuesday, pending flight availability.

Community Fundraising Enables the Operation

According to the article, a local effort in Pearland raised $350,000 to cover the costs associated with keeping the band and guard in New York during the postponement. The campaign was spearheaded by former mayor Kevin Cole, who coordinated donations and logistical support. The funds reportedly allowed United to provide the three charter‑style flights, ensuring that all participants could return home the same day the parade resumed.
