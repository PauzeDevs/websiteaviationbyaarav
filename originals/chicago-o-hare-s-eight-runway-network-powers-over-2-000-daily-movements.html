Chicago O’Hare’s Eight‑Runway Network Powers Over 2,000 Daily Movements

Simple Flying reports that ORD’s eight runways, most of them in a six‑parallel east‑west layout, enable more than 2,000 aircraft movements each day, making it the world’s busiest airport by movements, though the data remain uncorroborated by other outlets.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-20 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to Simple Flying, Chicago O’Hare International Airport (ORD) logged over 857,000 aircraft movements in 2025, averaging more than 2,000 takeoffs and landings per day. The airport’s eight runways—six aligned east‑west and two oriented southwest‑northeast—support a highly flexible traffic flow. The runway system, completed in 2021, allows up to four parallel approaches and multiple departure streams, but current operations are constrained more by departures than arrivals, suggesting capacity for additional flights.

Scale of Operations

Simple Flying notes that ORD recorded well over 2,000 aircraft movements each day during 2025, totaling more than 857,000 movements for the year. This volume places O’Hare at the top of global rankings when aircraft movements, rather than passenger numbers, are used as the metric. The publication emphasizes that such a high movement count reflects the airport’s role as a major hub for both commercial airlines and cargo operators.

Runway Layout and Recent Enhancements

The airport’s eight runways are organized into two primary orientations. Six runways run east‑west (designated 9L/27R, 9C/27C, 9R/27L, 10L/28R, 10C/28C, and 10R/28L), while two runways (4L/22R and 4R/22L) are aligned southwest‑northeast. The longer of the latter, runway 4R/22L, measures 8,075 feet (2,461 meters). Simple Flying explains that the two northeast‑southwest runways are typically reserved for strong crosswinds or atypical traffic patterns, leaving the six east‑west runways as the primary workhorses. The current configuration was finalized in 2021 after an expansion that extended runway 9R/27R and added runway 9C/27C, replacing an earlier seven‑runway arrangement.

Operational Flexibility

With the six parallel east‑west runways, air traffic controllers at ORD can theoretically manage up to four simultaneous parallel approaches and two parallel departures. In practice, the airport often runs two parallel departures, but the infrastructure permits greater flexibility. Simple Flying points out that, despite this capacity, the airport’s departure processes currently limit overall traffic more than arrival handling, indicating that the runway system could support a higher flight volume if departure sequencing were optimized.

Future Capacity Considerations

Given the existing runway geometry and the ability to run multiple parallel approaches, Simple Flying suggests that O’Hare could accommodate additional flights beyond the present schedule. However, the report does not provide specific forecasts or operational plans, and it acknowledges that the airport’s capacity is ultimately bounded by factors such as gate availability, ground handling resources, and airspace constraints beyond the runway complex.

What this means

The data supplied by Simple Flying illustrate how O’Hare’s extensive parallel runway system underpins its status as the busiest airport by movements. The six east‑west runways enable high‑density arrival and departure streams, while the two auxiliary runways provide resilience during adverse wind conditions. Because controllers can run multiple parallel approaches, the airport’s runway capacity exceeds its present operational use, with departure sequencing identified as the limiting factor. If gate and ground‑handling capacity were expanded, the existing runway infrastructure could support a measurable increase in daily movements.
