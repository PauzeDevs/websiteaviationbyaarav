Air France Extends Airbus A318 Service to March 2027, Spotting Opportunities Emerge

Air France’s last four A318s will continue operating domestic and intra‑European routes from Paris CDG until late March 2027, with the carrier gradually replacing them with A220‑300s.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-25 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that Air France has postponed the retirement of its Airbus A318 fleet from November 2026 to March 27, 2027. The four remaining aircraft are still based at Paris Charles de Gaulle and serve a handful of French and nearby European airports. Spotters can focus on CDG and secondary hubs such as Marseille, Toulouse, Bordeaux, Nice, Milan Linate and Lyon, where recent flight‑tracker data show the highest concentration of A318 movements. The airline is concurrently expanding its A220‑300 fleet, having taken delivery of its 60th unit in early November, signalling a phased phase‑out of the A318 despite the extended timetable. All details stem from Simple Flying and have not been independently verified.

Extended Service Timeline

According to Simple Flying, Air France originally planned to cease scheduled passenger use of the Airbus A318 on 8 November 2026. A subsequent schedule revision now lists 27 March 2027 as the final operating date shown in the airline’s published timetable. The carrier has not announced a definitive farewell flight, and aircraft assignments may still shift independently of the printed schedule, meaning the presence of an A318 in a reservation system does not guarantee that a passenger will travel on that type.

Current Operational Pattern

The four surviving A318s continue to operate out of Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG) on a mix of domestic French routes and short‑haul intra‑European services. Simple Flying’s flight‑tracker analysis of 423 A318 flights between 12 October and 8 November identified activity at 30 airports, with the most frequent departures and arrivals observed at CDG, Marseille (MRS), Toulouse (TLS), Bordeaux (BOD), Nice (NCE), Milan Linate (LIN) and Lyon (LYS). One of the four aircraft temporarily disappeared from the data after 20 October but later returned to service, underscoring the fluid nature of aircraft deployment.

Spotting Guidance

For aviation enthusiasts seeking to view the A318 before its eventual retirement, Simple Flying recommends focusing on the aforementioned airports, especially during the weeks leading up to the March deadline. The data suggests that recent movements are a more reliable indicator than advance schedule publications, as aircraft may be reassigned on short notice. Observers are advised to monitor real‑time flight‑tracking platforms for the latest position reports of the four remaining A318 registrations.

Fleet Modernisation

Air France is simultaneously accelerating the introduction of Airbus A220‑300s across its European network. The airline received its 60th A220 on 6 November, which entered service the following day. The A220‑300 seats 148 passengers, compared with the A318’s 131, and is being deployed on many routes previously served by the smaller A320 family member. This transition indicates a strategic move toward newer, more efficient aircraft even as the A318’s retirement is delayed.

What this means

The extension of the A318’s service window reflects Air France’s need to maintain capacity on niche routes while its newer A220 fleet scales up. By keeping the A318 operational for an additional five months, the airline avoids a sudden capacity gap, especially on lower‑density domestic legs. The data‑driven spotting advice highlights how flight‑tracker analytics can supplement traditional schedule information, offering a more dynamic picture of aircraft utilization. The gradual A220 rollout suggests that the A318’s retirement is part of a broader fleet renewal plan rather than an abrupt phase‑out.
