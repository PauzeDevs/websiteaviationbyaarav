UPS Boeing 767 freighter collides with Copa Airlines 737 on Miami taxiway

A taxiway incident at Miami International Airport left two crew members with minor injuries and prompted an FAA investigation, after the cargo aircraft failed to yield to the arriving passenger jet.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-15 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

On the night of 15 November 2026, a UPS Airlines Boeing 767‑300F and a Copa Airlines Boeing 737‑800 made contact while maneuvering on the ground at Miami International Airport. The collision occurred at an intersection of taxiways when the freighter advanced beyond its clearance, striking the 737’s left wingtip with its nose. Both aircraft halted on the taxiway, and two individuals received treatment for minor injuries. The Federal Aviation Administration has opened a formal investigation, and air traffic control communications captured the moment of impact. The incident follows a nationwide ground stop that had halted U.S. air travel the previous day.

Incident Overview

At approximately 21:15 local time, the two aircraft were taxiing on intersecting paths at Miami International Airport. The Copa flight, arriving from Panama City, was instructed by air traffic control to proceed to its gate, while the UPS flight was preparing for departure to Bogotá. Controllers directed the UPS crew to allow the 737 to pass, but the cargo plane continued forward, entering the 737’s trajectory. The 737’s left wingtip made contact with the nose of the freighter, causing both aircraft to stop on the taxiway.

Sequence of Events and Communications

Radio recordings released by an aviation monitoring service show the controller asking the UPS crew whether they had struck another aircraft. The UPS pilot acknowledged the error, stating they had gone too far. The 737 pilot reported damage to the left wingtip, describing it as missing. Following the collision, the controller instructed the Copa crew to hold position while fire and rescue units were dispatched to the scene.

Response, Injuries and Investigation

Emergency responders arrived promptly, and two people—one from each aircraft—were treated for minor injuries that did not require hospital transport. The FAA has opened an investigation into the ground collision, focusing on adherence to taxi instructions and situational awareness. No further details on aircraft damage have been released beyond the reported nose impact on the 767 and the missing wingtip segment on the 737.

Operational and Safety Implications

The incident underscores the importance of strict compliance with taxi clearances, especially in busy hub airports. It also highlights the role of real‑time communication between pilots and controllers in preventing ground mishaps. While the FAA’s inquiry is ongoing, the event may prompt a review of taxiway signage, clearance procedures, and crew training related to ground navigation.

What this means

The evidence points to a procedural lapse by the UPS flight crew, who failed to yield as instructed by ATC. The captured radio exchange confirms the crew’s acknowledgment of overshooting the taxiway limit. While the immediate physical impact was limited, the loss of a wingtip segment raises concerns about structural integrity and potential downstream maintenance costs. The incident may serve as a catalyst for reinforcing ground‑movement protocols, especially after the recent nationwide ground stop that likely heightened operational pressures.
