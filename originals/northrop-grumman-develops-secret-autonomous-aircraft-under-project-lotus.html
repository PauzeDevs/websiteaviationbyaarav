Northrop Grumman Develops Secret Autonomous Aircraft Under Project Lotus

A new uncrewed aircraft system has been identified at the Scaled Composites facility in Mojave, California, potentially signaling a bid for the U.S. Air Force's CCA Increment 2 program.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Aviation Week reports that Northrop Grumman has constructed a large, turbofan-powered uncrewed aircraft system (UAS) known as Project Lotus. The aircraft, currently housed at the Scaled Composites facility in Mojave, California, appears to be a contender for the U.S. Air Force's Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) Increment 2 initiative.

Discovery of Project Lotus

According to a report by Aviation Week, Northrop Grumman has secretly developed a large uncrewed aircraft system designated as Project Lotus. The aircraft was reportedly spotted at the Scaled Composites rapid prototyping facility located at the public airport in Mojave, California. Aviation Week states that it has reviewed a photograph of the completed, turbofan-powered demonstrator, though this account has not been independently corroborated.

Design Characteristics and Comparisons

Visual analysis of the aircraft suggests a design that shares some similarities with Lockheed Martin’s Project Vectis, specifically regarding the long, slender fuselage and a pointed, swept-back nose section. However, the two designs diverge significantly in other areas. While the Vectis features a low-mounted mid-fuselage engine inlet, the Lotus design places its inlet high on the extreme aft section of the fuselage. Furthermore, the Lotus incorporates sharply canted tails, a departure from the tailless configuration observed on the Vectis.

Strategic Context and Future Outlook

The emergence of Project Lotus is widely viewed as Northrop Grumman’s entry for the U.S. Air Force’s Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) Increment 2 program. The Air Force is currently in the process of finalizing requirements for this initiative, with a competitive prototype acquisition phase expected to commence in the coming year. The existence of a built demonstrator suggests that Northrop Grumman is positioning itself to meet these upcoming requirements.
