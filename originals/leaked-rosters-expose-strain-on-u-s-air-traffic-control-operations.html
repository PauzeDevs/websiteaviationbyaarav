Leaked Rosters Expose Strain on U.S. Air Traffic Control Operations

Internal scheduling documents reveal mandatory overtime and extended shift rotations as the FAA grapples with a significant controller deficit.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-15 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Leaked scheduling data indicates that U.S. air traffic controllers are facing grueling work patterns, including 14-day consecutive duty cycles and mandatory overtime, amid a national staffing shortage.

Evidence of Extended Duty Cycles

According to a report by Simple Flying, which cites documents obtained by The Washington Examiner, air traffic controller rosters reveal a pattern of extreme scheduling. One specific roster analyzed showed a controller working for 14 consecutive days, with only a nominal break following a Saturday night shift. These schedules frequently incorporate mandatory overtime, effectively extending standard five-day rotations into six-day workweeks. The rosters also highlight the use of 'Rattler Shifts,' which involve rotating start times that shift from afternoon hours to early mornings within a short timeframe.

Staffing Deficits and Operational Impact

The reported scheduling pressures are attributed to a national shortfall of more than 3,000 air traffic controllers. Simple Flying reports that staffing levels at 20 of the 26 busiest U.S. airports and facilities have fallen below the 85% minimum threshold. This chronic understaffing has necessitated the implementation of mandatory overtime to maintain operational continuity. The current environment follows a period of tension between controllers and the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) regarding compensation, specifically involving a 2.8% pay increase that has reportedly been stalled by FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford.

Fatigue Management and Regulatory Constraints

The intensity of these work schedules has raised concerns regarding controller fatigue. To mitigate risks, current protocols mandate a minimum of ten hours of rest between standard shifts, increasing to 12 hours before specific duty types. Despite these requirements, the reliance on consecutive 10-hour shifts and mandatory overtime suggests that the system is operating under significant strain. These claims regarding the severity of the scheduling practices and the specific impact on controller fatigue are attributed to Simple Flying and have not been independently corroborated.

Key facts

  • A national shortage of over 3,000 air traffic controllers is currently impacting U.S. aviation operations.
  • Staffing levels at 20 of the 26 busiest U.S. airports are reportedly below the 85% minimum threshold.
  • Leaked rosters show controllers working 14 consecutive days with mandatory overtime.
  • Current fatigue protocols require at least 10 to 12 hours of rest between shifts.

What this means

The leaked scheduling data suggests that the FAA is utilizing mandatory overtime as a primary mechanism to compensate for a systemic shortage of personnel. The reliance on 'Rattler Shifts' and back-to-back duty days indicates that operational requirements are consistently pushing against the boundaries of established fatigue management protocols, potentially creating a precarious environment for air traffic safety.

What we could not verify

The specific rosters mentioned have not been independently verified, and the extent to which these schedules represent the broader national workforce remains unclear.
