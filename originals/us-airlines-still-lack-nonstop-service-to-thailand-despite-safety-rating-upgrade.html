US airlines still lack nonstop service to Thailand despite safety rating upgrade

Simple Flying notes that no US carrier operates a direct flight to Bangkok, citing the recent Category 1 safety rating and United’s one‑stop service as the only current link between the two markets.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-17 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to Simple Flying, the United States has no nonstop flights to Thailand. The article attributes the absence to a combination of historical safety‑rating restrictions, market demand patterns, and airline network strategies. Thailand’s safety rating was upgraded from Category 2 to Category 1 in April 2025, after which United launched a daily one‑stop service via Hong Kong using a Boeing 787‑9. While US traveler interest in Thailand is rising, the article suggests that additional nonstop routes are unlikely in the near term.

Background

Simple Flying highlights Thailand’s status as a top global tourist destination, recording over 35 million international arrivals in 2024. The primary source markets for those visitors are China and India, with US travelers representing a smaller but slowly growing segment. The article points out that despite the country’s appeal, no US airline currently offers a direct flight between any US city and Bangkok’s Suvarnabhumi Airport.

Regulatory Landscape

The piece explains that a 2015 audit found Thailand non‑compliant with International Civil Aviation Organization safety standards, prompting the FAA to assign a Category 2 rating that barred Thai carriers from operating directly to the United States. Simple Flying reports that Thailand’s Deputy Prime Minister and Transport Minister, Suriya Jungrungreangkit, expressed confidence in regaining a Category 1 rating, which the article confirms occurred in April 2025.

Current Service Options

Following the rating upgrade, United Airlines introduced a one‑stop service linking Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) to Bangkok (BKK) via Hong Kong International Airport (HKG). Simple Flying notes that the route began operations in October 2025 with a Boeing 787‑9 and runs daily. United’s senior vice‑president of Global Network Planning & Alliances, Patrick Quayle, is quoted as emphasizing the airline’s aim to broaden travel options, though the article makes clear that the service remains a stop‑over rather than a nonstop connection.

Future Prospects

Simple Flying poses the question of whether the Category 1 rating will spur additional nonstop flights. The article concludes that, despite the regulatory improvement, there is no immediate expectation of new direct services. It suggests that market factors, such as the relative size of the US traveler base compared with Asian source markets, and airline network economics, continue to limit the incentive for carriers to launch nonstop routes at this time.

Key facts

  • Thailand received a Category 1 safety rating from the FAA in April 2025 after a prior downgrade in 2015.
  • United Airlines operates a daily one‑stop LAX‑Hong Kong‑Bangkok service using a Boeing 787‑9, launched in October 2025.
  • No US airline currently offers a nonstop flight to Thailand, according to Simple Flying.

What this means

The evidence indicates that regulatory clearance alone is insufficient to generate nonstop US‑Thailand flights. United’s decision to employ a stop‑over reflects a cautious approach to market entry, balancing demand uncertainty with the desire to test the route. Until US passenger volumes to Thailand reach a level that justifies the economics of a nonstop operation, airlines are likely to continue relying on hub‑and‑spoke models.
