FedEx MD-11 Overruns Miami Runway, Prompting FAA Ground Stop

A 34‑year‑old FedEx Express MD‑11 skidded off the runway at Miami International Airport on Nov. 25, 2026, triggering a temporary ground halt and extensive delays during the Thanksgiving travel surge.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-26 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reported that a FedEx Express McDonnell Douglas MD‑11 overran the runway while landing at Miami International Airport on the evening of Nov. 25, 2026. The incident led the Federal Aviation Administration to impose a ground stop, reducing runway availability and causing multi‑hour delays for hundreds of flights. Weather radar indicated rain at the time, and the airport’s communications infrastructure experienced disruptions. The FAA is leading the investigation, while FedEx is cooperating with authorities. No injuries were reported.

Incident Overview

According to Simple Flying, a FedEx Express MD‑11, registration FX910, touched down on a Miami runway and subsequently left the paved surface. The aircraft, described as 34 years old, came to rest off the runway, prompting immediate safety actions by the FAA. The airline confirmed it is working with investigators, but no further technical details about the aircraft’s condition have been released.

Weather and Communication Challenges

The report notes that weather radar showed rain in the vicinity of the airport at the time of the landing. Additional accounts cited by Simple Flying describe severe turbulence reports and a loss of land‑line communications with surrounding facilities, which may have compounded the difficulty of the approach. A journalist referenced in the article described the regional weather as a "black hole," reflecting the intensity of the storm system.

Operational Impact

Following the overrun, the FAA ordered a ground stop that limited runway usage to a single active strip for arrivals and a second for departures. The south runway handled all inbound traffic, while the diagonal runway reopened later in the evening for departures only. The partial shutdown extended average delay times from roughly 47 minutes shortly after the event to more than three and a half hours by early Thursday, according to an FAA advisory cited by Simple Flying. Over 530 flights were delayed as of 11:40 p.m. on Nov. 25, and the airport remained below full capacity through the night.

Investigation and Response

The FAA has taken the lead on the investigation, with FedEx Express confirming cooperation. No injuries were reported, and the aircraft’s occupants were unharmed. Simple Flying indicated that the incident occurred less than three months after a separate runway overrun involving an Amazon‑branded Boeing 767‑300F at the same airport, which resulted in fatalities. The proximity of the two events has heightened scrutiny of runway safety at Miami International Airport during peak travel periods.

The timing—during the Thanksgiving travel peak—exacerbated the operational fallout, turning a single aircraft incident into a system‑wide delay. The recurrence of runway overruns at Miami International Airport within a short span may prompt regulators to review approach procedures, runway drainage, and ATC communication resilience under severe weather conditions.
