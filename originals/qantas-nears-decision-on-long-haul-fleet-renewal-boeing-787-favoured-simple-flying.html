Qantas Nears Decision on Long‑Haul Fleet Renewal, Boeing 787 Favoured, Simple Flying Reports

According to Simple Flying, the Australian flag carrier is close to finalising a roughly 20‑aircraft wide‑body order that would lean heavily on Boeing’s 787 Dreamliner, with Airbus A350s filling the balance, though no formal commitment has been announced.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-27 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying cites anonymous sources indicating Qantas is poised to place an order for about 20 new wide‑body twinjets, primarily Boeing 787 Dreamliners, with a smaller share of Airbus A350s. The airline’s spokesperson confirmed discussions are ongoing but no decision has been made. The move aligns with Qantas’s broader fleet renewal strategy, which includes retiring its A380 fleet amid rising fuel costs linked to geopolitical tensions.

Fleet Renewal Context

Qantas, a founding member of the oneworld alliance, has been navigating a comprehensive wide‑body fleet renewal. The airline currently operates a mixed fleet that includes Boeing 787 Dreamliners and a pending order for Airbus A350‑1000 and A350‑1000ULR variants. Simple Flying notes that the A350 orders, totalling 12 standard and 12 upgraded aircraft, have not yet entered service. Concurrently, Qantas plans to retire its double‑deck Airbus A380s, a step the source attributes to the need for greater fuel efficiency as fuel prices rise in response to the war in Iran.

Reported Order Details

The Simple Flying article, referencing Bloomberg’s reporting, states that Qantas is weighing an order of approximately 20 new wide‑body twinjets. Anonymous sources close to the matter suggest the bulk of the order will be Boeing 787 Dreamliners, with the remaining aircraft likely to be Airbus A350s. No specific breakdown of numbers between the two types is provided, nor are delivery timelines disclosed. The report frames the decision as a defining long‑haul debate of the 21st century, reflecting broader industry trends toward fuel‑efficient, twin‑engine aircraft.

Manufacturer Preference and Strategic Rationale

According to the source, Qantas’s inclination toward the 787 stems partly from operational familiarity; the carrier has been an established 787 operator for several years. The Dreamliner’s fuel‑efficiency and range capabilities align with Qantas’s upcoming “Project Sunrise” ambitions, which aim to launch non‑stop flights from Sydney to London. The Airbus A350, particularly the ultra‑long‑range (ULR) variant, is also positioned to support these routes, but the source indicates a relative preference for the 787 in the pending order.

Official Response and Next Steps

When approached for comment, a Qantas spokesperson reiterated that discussions with aircraft manufacturers are ongoing and that no final decisions have been made. The airline’s statement, as quoted by Simple Flying, underscores that the fleet planning process remains in the negotiation phase. The source does not provide a timeline for when a formal order might be announced, nor does it detail any financial terms associated with the potential deal.

  • The remaining aircraft in the prospective order are expected to be Airbus A350 models, according to anonymous sources.
  • Qantas currently holds orders for 12 Airbus A350‑1000s and 12 A350‑1000ULRs, none of which have entered service yet.

What this means

If Qantas proceeds with a predominantly Boeing 787 order, the airline would deepen its operational reliance on a platform it already operates, potentially simplifying crew training, maintenance, and parts logistics. The inclusion of Airbus A350s, particularly the ULR variant, would preserve flexibility for ultra‑long‑range routes such as Project Sunrise, where the A350‑ULR’s range advantage could be decisive. The retirement of A380s reflects a broader industry shift toward more fuel‑efficient twin‑engine aircraft, a trend accelerated by higher fuel prices tied to geopolitical events. The mixed‑fleet approach could allow Qantas to balance cost efficiency with route‑specific performance requirements, but it also introduces complexity in fleet management that the airline will need to address through careful planning.
