JSX Acquires New Pacific Airlines, Expands Capacity Beyond 30‑Seat Semi‑Private Model

Simple Flying reports that JSX purchased the bankrupt New Pacific Airlines for roughly $3.1 million, gaining a Part 121 certificate and a pathway to operate 42‑seat Embraer ERJ145‑LR jets, marking a strategic shift from its existing 30‑seat charter framework.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-24 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

JSX’s purchase of New Pacific Airlines, announced after a Department of Transportation filing, provides the carrier with a Part 121 operating certificate and the ability to deploy larger 42‑seat Embraer jets from its existing fleet. The deal, valued at about $3.1 million, is expected to see two aircraft in service initially, expanding to six within five months, and to generate over 10,000 flights and roughly 309,000 passenger boardings in the first year. The move also offers regulatory flexibility amid heightened FAA scrutiny of public‑charter operators.

Background and Rationale

According to Simple Flying, JSX has long operated under a Part 135 framework that limits aircraft to 30 passenger seats, even though its Embraer fleet is capable of carrying more. The carrier’s ambition to broaden its premium regional offering prompted it to seek a Part 121 certificate, which would lift the seat‑capacity restriction. New Pacific Airlines, which had halted a planned Boeing 757 service in November 2025 and entered bankruptcy, presented a ready‑made certificate and a regulatory pathway for JSX to pursue larger aircraft without introducing new types.

Acquisition Terms and Fleet Plan

The Simple Flying report states that JSX agreed to purchase New Pacific for approximately $3.1 million. The transaction, completed in August 2026 but disclosed publicly after a DOT filing on November 20, includes the transfer of New Pacific’s FAA Part 121 operating authority. JSX intends to re‑brand the acquired entity as JSX Air and initially operate two Embraer ERJ145‑LR jets, leased from its sister company Delux Public Charter. The carrier projects an expansion to six such aircraft by the fifth month of operation. Each ERJ145‑LR will be configured for 42 seats, a 40 % increase over JSX’s current 30‑seat layout.

Projected Operational Impact

Simple Flying cites JSX’s internal projections of 10,106 flights and about 309,000 passenger boardings in the first year following the acquisition. The increased capacity is expected to enhance the airline’s ability to serve higher‑density regional routes while maintaining its semi‑private service model. The use of existing aircraft from JSX’s fleet, rather than acquiring new types, is presented as a cost‑effective way to scale up operations.

Regulatory Landscape

The report notes that the FAA has been considering tighter oversight of public‑charter operators, a development previously highlighted by AP News. By moving to a Part 121 certificate, JSX may gain greater regulatory flexibility and reduce exposure to potential restrictions associated with its current Part 380 public‑charter framework. However, the transition will still require FAA approval for the larger seating configuration and continued compliance with Part 121 standards.

Key facts

  • JSX purchased bankrupt New Pacific Airlines for roughly $3.1 million.
  • The deal provides JSX with a Part 121 operating certificate, enabling 42‑seat aircraft.
  • Initial service will use two ERJ145‑LR jets, expanding to six within five months.
  • Projected first‑year activity includes 10,106 flights and about 309,000 passenger boardings.
  • The acquisition follows New Pacific’s abandonment of a Boeing 757 plan in November 2025.

What this means

The acquisition signals JSX’s intent to evolve from a niche semi‑private charter model toward a more conventional regional airline, leveraging existing assets to increase seat capacity while preserving its premium brand positioning. Gaining a Part 121 certificate may also insulate the carrier from forthcoming FAA regulatory tightening aimed at public‑charter operators, though final approval for the larger configuration remains pending.
