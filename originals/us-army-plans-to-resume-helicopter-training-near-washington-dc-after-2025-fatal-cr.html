US Army Plans to Resume Helicopter Training Near Washington DC After 2025 Fatal Crash

The service seeks FAA approval to restart Black Hawk training flights in the restricted Potomac corridor the week of Dec. 6, 2026, two years after a mid‑air collision killed 67 people.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-25 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

The US Army intends to restart helicopter training operations in the airspace surrounding Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport after a two‑year suspension triggered by a deadly 2025 mid‑air collision and a subsequent near‑miss incident. The move, reported by Simple Flying and based on a Politico briefing, hinges on regulatory clearance from the Federal Aviation Administration, which has not yet received a formal request. Existing FAA rules, enacted in early 2026, limit routine training in the designated Potomac zone to essential missions only, creating a procedural hurdle for the Army’s planned proficiency flights.

2025 Mid‑Air Collision and Immediate Aftermath

On Jan. 29, 2025, an Army Sikorsky UH‑60 Black Hawk collided with a PSA Airlines regional jet operating as American Airlines Flight 5342 over the Potomac River. The accident claimed all 67 occupants on both aircraft. In the months that followed, a separate incident in May 2025 saw a Black Hawk approach too close to Reagan National Airport, forcing commercial arrivals to abort landings. Those events prompted the Army to halt all training flights in the corridor pending safety reviews.

Regulatory Landscape Shaping the Resumption

The FAA’s interim final rule 2026‑01226, effective Jan. 23, 2026, permanently closed Helicopter Route 4 and restricts entry into the Potomac zone to flights classified as essential—such as medical evacuations, law‑enforcement actions, national‑defense missions, continuity‑of‑government operations, or transport of the President or Vice President. Routine proficiency checks and non‑essential personnel movements are expressly excluded. The rule opened for public comment through Feb. 23, 2026, and a final rule is expected after the comment period. Because routine training does not meet the essential‑flight criteria, the Army would need either a regulatory amendment or a specific authorization to conduct standard currency flights.

Planned Timeline and Current Status

According to the Simple Flying report, Army officials briefed congressional staff on a plan to resume training flights the week of Dec. 6, 2026. The timeline assumes that the FAA will grant the necessary clearance, although the service has not yet filed a formal request with the agency. No details on any additional safety measures or procedural changes have been disclosed in the source material.

Implications for Airspace Management

If approved, the resumption would re‑introduce military rotorcraft into one of the nation’s most tightly controlled airspaces. The FAA would need to balance the Army’s training requirements with the safety of commercial traffic operating out of Reagan National. The situation underscores the broader tension between military readiness and civilian airspace protection, especially in densely populated corridors.

What this means

The Army’s intent to restart training reflects a push to restore pilot currency after a prolonged safety pause, but the effort is constrained by recent FAA regulations that prioritize essential operations in the Potomac corridor. Without a formal waiver or rule change, the Army may be limited to conducting only essential flights, which could impede routine proficiency checks. The pending FAA decision will likely set a precedent for how military training is accommodated in high‑traffic civilian airspace.
