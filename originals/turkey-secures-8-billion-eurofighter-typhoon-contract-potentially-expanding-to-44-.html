Turkey Secures £8 billion Eurofighter Typhoon Contract, Potentially Expanding to 44 Aircraft

Aviation Week reports that Turkey has signed a deal for 20 new‑build Eurofighter Typhoons, with the defence minister hinting at a further 24 second‑hand jets from Oman and Qatar, a move that could reshape Ankara’s fighter fleet by the early 2030s.

At a glance

Turkey has entered a long‑awaited procurement agreement with the Eurofighter consortium, valued at £8 billion ($11 billion) for 20 newly built Typhoons. The contract marks the first export of the four‑nation fighter since 2017 and could grow to include 24 additional second‑hand aircraft from Oman and Qatar, according to Turkish Defence Minister Yasar Guler. Deliveries of the new jets are slated for 2030, while potential second‑hand deliveries could arrive as early as next year, accelerating the retirement of legacy platforms such as the F‑4 Phantom. BAE Systems is slated to receive £5.4 billion of the contract value, covering aircraft and a weapons package from MBDA, with the remaining £2.6 billion not detailed in the source.

Deal Overview and Financial Scope

Aviation Week states that Turkey signed an £8 billion contract to acquire 20 brand‑new Eurofighter Typhoons, the first major export of the programme since 2017. The agreement represents Ankara’s inaugural purchase of a European‑designed combat aircraft after decades of operating U.S.‑origin fighters. BAE Systems, the primary contractor, is expected to receive £5.4 billion of the total, encompassing the airframes and a weapons suite supplied by MBDA. The source notes that the remaining £2.6 billion of the contract value is not itemised in the excerpt.

Potential Expansion via Second‑Hand Acquisitions

Turkish Defence Minister Yasar Guler indicated that the initial order could more than double if Turkey secures an additional 24 second‑hand Typhoons—12 each from Oman and Qatar. Guler suggested that Qatar might be able to deliver its aircraft as early as next year, which would allow Turkey to introduce the Eurofighter into service ahead of the 2030 schedule for the new‑build jets. The minister’s comments also imply that Oman would be relinquishing its own Eurofighter fleet to facilitate the transfer.

Operational Impact and Industrial Implications

If the combined procurement proceeds, the Eurofighter would replace older platforms in the Turkish Air Force, notably the McDonnell Douglas F‑4 Phantom, which the source cites as slated for retirement. Integration work on the weapons package may involve Turkish indigenous armaments, although the extent of such localisation is not detailed. The deal underscores BAE Systems’ role in the programme, with production slated for the Warton facility in England. The inclusion of MBDA’s weapons package suggests a comprehensive capability package, but the source does not specify the exact missile types or support equipment.

Strategic Context and Regional Considerations

The transaction marks a shift in Turkey’s defence procurement strategy toward European platforms, diverging from its historical reliance on U.S. aircraft. The potential transfer of second‑hand Typhoons from Oman and Qatar could affect the operational postures of those nations, as the source notes that Oman would be giving up its Eurofighter operations. However, the article provides no further detail on how these states plan to replace the transferred aircraft.

The possibility of acquiring second‑hand Typhoons from Oman and Qatar could accelerate the fleet’s operational entry, allowing older platforms to be retired sooner than the 2030 timeline for the new builds. From an industrial perspective, BAE Systems stands to secure a substantial portion of the contract value, reinforcing its role as the primary Eurofighter supplier.
