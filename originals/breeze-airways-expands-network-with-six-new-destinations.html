Breeze Airways Expands Network with Six New Destinations

The Utah-based carrier is filling service gaps left by Spirit Airlines while expanding its international footprint in the Caribbean.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-04 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Breeze Airways is set to launch service to six new destinations, focusing on secondary markets and routes previously vacated by Spirit Airlines, including new international links to Jamaica and the Dominican Republic.

Strategic Network Expansion

Breeze Airways, the Utah-based carrier founded by David Neeleman, is continuing its strategy of connecting secondary airports and unserved markets. According to a report by Simple Flying, the airline is preparing to add six new destinations to its network in the coming months. This expansion is characterized by a mix of domestic route growth and the introduction of new international services, specifically targeting locations in Jamaica and the Dominican Republic.

Filling the Void Left by Spirit

A significant portion of this growth involves the airline reclaiming routes that were left unserved following the collapse of Spirit Airlines earlier this year. Simple Flying reports that Breeze is actively stepping into these gaps, most notably at Myrtle Beach International Airport (MYR), where it has become one of the largest carriers by number of destinations served. The airline plans to reconnect Myrtle Beach with Latrobe (LBE) and Boston Logan International Airport (BOS) starting in February and May 2026, respectively.

Operational Details and Market Impact

The airline's expansion includes the reintroduction of commercial air service at Latrobe, Pennsylvania, which had been abandoned by Spirit. Breeze is scheduled to begin flights from Latrobe to Myrtle Beach on February 9, 2026, and to Orlando International Airport (MCO) on May 26, 2026. While the route to Myrtle Beach from Latrobe faces no direct competition, the service between Myrtle Beach and Boston will compete with established carriers including American Airlines and Delta Air Lines. Breeze currently operates a fleet consisting of 66 Airbus A220-300 aircraft.

Key facts

  • Breeze Airways is adding six new destinations to its network.
  • The airline is utilizing its fleet of 66 Airbus A220-300 aircraft for this expansion.
  • New routes include service from Latrobe to Myrtle Beach and Orlando, and from Myrtle Beach to Boston.
  • The expansion includes new international service to Jamaica and the Dominican Republic.

What this means

Breeze Airways is leveraging the market instability caused by the exit of Spirit Airlines to solidify its position in secondary leisure markets. By focusing on point-to-point connectivity and avoiding direct competition on several of its new routes, the airline is attempting to capture demand in underserved regions while simultaneously diversifying its portfolio with international Caribbean destinations.
