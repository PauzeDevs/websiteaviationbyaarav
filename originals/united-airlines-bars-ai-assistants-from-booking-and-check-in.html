United Airlines Bars AI Assistants from Booking and Check‑In

The carrier adds a clause to its Contract of Carriage prohibiting third‑party AI tools from creating, altering or servicing tickets, citing data‑security and regulatory risks.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-12 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

United Airlines announced that it will no longer allow external artificial‑intelligence agents to book flights, modify reservations or complete check‑in procedures on its digital platforms. The policy, incorporated into a revised Contract of Carriage, gives the airline the right to cancel any reservation handled by such tools. United says the move protects passenger data and ensures compliance with FAA and DOT rules.

Policy Overview

United’s updated Contract of Carriage introduces Rule 6.J.6, which explicitly lists the use of AI agents—ranging from voice assistants to autonomous browsing scripts—as a prohibited ticketing practice. The airline states that the restriction applies whether the AI operates independently or under a passenger’s supervision. In addition, Rule 5.H grants United the authority to void any reservation that was created, altered, or serviced by an AI tool, regardless of whether the passenger later approves the transaction.

Rationale and Regulatory Concerns

According to United, third‑party AI assistants could expose sensitive account credentials and lead to unintended purchases or mileage usage. The carrier also warns that automated check‑in actions might bypass mandatory passenger confirmations about prohibited items, potentially violating Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and Department of Transportation (DOT) regulations. United’s statement links these concerns to the broader risk of non‑human agents performing tasks that are legally required to be confirmed by a human traveler.

Impact on Passengers and Travel Platforms

The ban covers United’s website, mobile app, and any third‑party booking sites that distribute its inventory. Passengers who currently rely on AI‑driven services—such as chat‑based travel planners or browser extensions that auto‑fill booking forms—will need to complete transactions manually. United notes that the policy does not affect the airline’s internal automation, only external, user‑directed AI tools. Travel agencies and aggregators are instructed to enforce the restriction within their own systems to avoid reservation cancellations.

Industry Context

The move follows a report that Delta Air Lines recently began limiting similar AI tools, though United is the first U.S. carrier to formalise the restriction in its contract language. Simple Flying’s coverage cites a Wall Street Journal report as the original source of the announcement, but no other outlets have independently verified the details at the time of writing.

Key facts

  • United adds Rule 6.J.6 to its Contract of Carriage, banning AI agents from making or modifying tickets
  • Rule 5.H gives United the right to cancel reservations handled by AI tools
  • The policy applies to United’s website, app and third‑party booking platforms
  • United cites data‑security and FAA/DOT compliance as reasons for the ban

What this means

United’s decision reflects growing corporate caution around the use of generative AI in consumer‑facing processes. By embedding the restriction in its contract, the airline creates a clear legal basis to invalidate AI‑generated bookings, which could deter third‑party developers from integrating United’s inventory into autonomous agents. The emphasis on regulatory compliance suggests that the airline anticipates potential enforcement scrutiny if AI tools bypass mandatory passenger acknowledgments, especially during check‑in. While the policy may inconvenience tech‑savvy travelers, it also signals a broader industry trend toward tighter control of digital interactions that involve personal data and safety‑critical confirmations.
