AutoFlight Unveils V5000 Matrix eVTOL for Regional Operations

The newly revealed 10-seat hybrid-electric aircraft aims to expand the operational scope of vertical take-off and landing technology.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

AutoFlight Europe GmbH has introduced the V5000 Matrix, a high-capacity eVTOL aircraft designed for regional travel. Featuring hybrid-electric capabilities and a 10-seat configuration, the aircraft is currently undergoing flight testing as the company pursues certification under CAAC powered-lift regulations.

Development and Flight Testing

AutoFlight Europe GmbH officially unveiled the V5000 Matrix in February 2026. According to a report by Aviation Week, the aircraft has already commenced flight testing, successfully completing a remotely piloted transition from thrustborne hover to wingborne cruise. While the manufacturer has not provided a formal timeline for commercial availability, industry observers have speculated that the aircraft could potentially enter service between 2027 and 2028. These claims regarding entry-into-service dates have not been independently corroborated.

Design and Configuration

The V5000 Matrix represents a structural evolution of AutoFlight’s earlier Prosperity I and CarryAll models. It is designed as a lift-plus-cruise hybrid-electric or all-electric aircraft with a maximum take-off weight of 5,700kg for the hybrid variant. The cabin is notably spacious, offering configurations for six passengers in a VIP layout or nine passengers plus a pilot in a business arrangement. A significant design feature is the inclusion of an onboard lavatory, which the manufacturer suggests reflects the aircraft's intended use for longer-duration regional flights.

Market Strategy and Certification

The V5000 Matrix is positioned to serve a regional market, distinguishing it from smaller, urban-focused eVTOL designs. In addition to passenger transport, AutoFlight is developing an uncrewed cargo variant of the platform. The company is currently seeking certification from the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC) under powered-lift regulations. The aircraft features a high-wing design, panoramic windows, and skid-style landing gear to support its operational requirements.

Key facts

  • The V5000 Matrix is a 10-seat eVTOL aircraft capable of hybrid-electric or all-electric operation.
  • The aircraft has successfully completed a remotely piloted transition from hover to wingborne cruise.
  • AutoFlight is seeking certification from the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC).
  • The design includes a lavatory, a first for the eVTOL sector, signaling a focus on longer flight durations.

What this means

The V5000 Matrix signals a strategic shift in the eVTOL sector toward regional connectivity rather than purely urban air mobility. By incorporating a lavatory and increasing passenger capacity to 10, AutoFlight is attempting to bridge the gap between traditional rotorcraft and regional fixed-wing aircraft. However, the success of this model will depend heavily on its ability to meet stringent CAAC powered-lift certification standards, which remain a significant hurdle for high-capacity electric aircraft.
