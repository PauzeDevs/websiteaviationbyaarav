Severe hailstorm prompts inspections of over two dozen American Airlines jets at Philadelphia International Airport

A sudden hail event in the Philadelphia region forced ground stops, extensive aircraft inspections and widespread flight delays across the Northeast, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-13 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

On 13 November 2026 a strong hailstorm swept through Delaware County and surrounding parts of Pennsylvania, covering Philadelphia International Airport in a layer of ice. Simple Flying reports that the weather forced American Airlines to hold roughly two dozen aircraft for visual and technical checks of windows, radomes and engines before they could resume service. The incident triggered a ground stop at PHL, contributed to more than 800 delayed flights nationwide and 65 cancellations, and added to weather‑related disruptions at several major Northeast airports.

Weather event and immediate airport response

Simple Flying describes the hailstorm as unusually intense, leaving the city and the airport with a white‑out appearance. Aviation insider JonNYC, cited in the report, noted that the sudden front caused American Airlines to hold aircraft shortly after the hail struck, giving maintenance crews time to examine each plane for hail damage. The airline’s engineers focused on vulnerable components such as cockpit windows, nose radomes and engine inlets before granting clearance for departure.

Operational impact on American Airlines

According to the Simple Flying article, the hold resulted in a ground stop that affected only American‑operated flights at Philadelphia. The airline’s fleet at the airport included more than two dozen jets that required inspection. While the report does not specify exact aircraft types, the emphasis was on ensuring that no hail‑induced cracks or dents compromised airworthiness. The ground stop contributed to a cascade of delays, with the article citing a total of 820 delayed flights across the United States and 65 cancellations, figures that were attributed to Oregon Live.

Wider regional disruptions

The hailstorm coincided with low cloud ceilings, persistent rain and wind across the Northeast, further hampering airport operations. Simple Flying lists several major airports that experienced service degradation, including Boston (BOS), Chicago O'Hare (ORD), Chicago Midway (MDW), New York LaGuardia (LGA), New York JFK (JFK), Newark (EWR), Washington Dulles (IAD), Washington National (DCA) and Baltimore (BWI). The combination of adverse weather and the Philadelphia incident amplified passenger inconvenience, with thousands of travelers facing late‑arriving connections and altered itineraries.

Safety protocols and industry response

The article underscores that the inspections were conducted in line with standard safety procedures that require visual and functional checks after severe weather exposure. While the piece does not detail any specific findings, the prompt grounding and examination reflect the industry’s precautionary stance toward hail, which can cause hidden structural damage. No reports of injuries or aircraft damage beyond the inspections were mentioned.

What this means

The incident illustrates how localized extreme weather can quickly cascade into system‑wide operational challenges. By grounding aircraft for inspections, American Airlines adhered to safety-first protocols, preventing potential in‑flight failures. However, the ground stop amplified an already strained Northeast airspace, where concurrent weather fronts were affecting multiple hubs. The reported delay and cancellation numbers suggest that even a single airport’s weather event can have measurable ripple effects on national flight schedules, highlighting the importance of robust contingency planning and real‑time coordination among airlines, airports and air traffic control.
