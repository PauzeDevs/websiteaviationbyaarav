EHang Advances VT35 eVTOL Toward Flight Testing and Certification

Aviation Week reports that Chinese manufacturer EHang has finished assembling its two‑seat VT35 lift‑plus‑cruise eVTOL, begun flight‑test campaigns, and outlined a CAAC certification path aimed for late‑2025.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-12 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to Aviation Week, EHang’s VT35 autonomous eVTOL has moved from assembly to active flight testing, with a public demonstration slated for December 2025. The aircraft features a distributed electric propulsion layout, a two‑passenger cabin with panoramic windows, and a projected range of roughly 200 km. Certification will be pursued with China’s civil aviation authority, leveraging experience from the earlier EH216‑S model.

Prototype Completion and Test Schedule

Aviation Week states that the VT35’s first prototype has reached the final assembly stage and is currently undergoing flight‑test evaluations. The publisher notes that the company plans a public flight demonstration in December 2025, marking the first external showcase of the vehicle’s autonomous capabilities.

Design Architecture and Propulsion

The VT35 is described as a lift‑plus‑cruise configuration that seats two occupants in a cabin fitted with wide‑angle viewing windows. Its lift system consists of eight electrically driven fans mounted on tandem wings—four oriented upward and four downward—to provide vertical take‑off and landing. A rear‑mounted pusher propeller supplies forward thrust for cruise. Each fan operates with its own motor, a layout termed distributed electric propulsion (DEP), which the source claims offers redundancy by eliminating single points of failure.

Performance Targets and Certification Outlook

Published specifications list a maximum take‑off weight of 2,094 lb (950 kg) and a payload capacity of 440 lb (200 kg). The airframe measures roughly 26 ft (8 m) in both length and wingspan. While cruising altitude and speed remain undisclosed, the VT35 is projected to achieve a range of about 124 miles (200 km). Certification is expected to be pursued with the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC), with the publisher suggesting an accelerated timeline based on EHang’s prior certification experience with the EH216‑S model.

Potential Market Impact

If the VT35 meets its development milestones, it could add a two‑seat, autonomous eVTOL option to the emerging urban air mobility market. The combination of DEP redundancy and a compact passenger cabin aligns with safety and convenience priorities cited by industry observers, though the source does not provide market forecasts or operator interest data.

What this means

The VT35 represents EHang’s next step in autonomous eVTOL development, moving from design to operational testing. The distributed propulsion scheme, highlighted by the source, is intended to enhance safety through redundancy, a critical factor for regulatory acceptance. By targeting a public flight in late 2025 and pursuing an accelerated certification path, EHang appears to be positioning the VT35 for early entry into China’s urban air mobility ecosystem, where regulatory familiarity with its earlier models may provide a competitive edge.
