United’s 43‑inch pitch seats on Boeing 767‑400ER serve as crew‑rest, not passenger luxury

Simple Flying reports that four unusually spacious seats on United’s 767‑400ER aircraft are intended for flight‑attendant rest, though they are sometimes offered to passengers on shorter routes

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-05 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to a Simple Flying article, United Airlines equips each of its sixteen Boeing 767‑400ERs with four seats that provide 43 inches of pitch—far beyond the airline’s standard economy spacing. The seats are positioned in rows 52 and 53 on the left side of the cabin and are primarily used as dedicated crew‑rest stations on long‑haul flights. On routes that do not require a crew‑rest area, United may open the seats to passenger booking, creating a rare opportunity for travelers to experience premium‑level legroom in economy. The report is the sole source for these details and has not been independently verified.

Seat configuration and dimensions

Simple Flying describes the four seats as occupying the rear left side of the economy cabin on all United 767‑400ERs. They are located in rows 52 and 53, seats A and B, and feature a seat pitch of 43 inches—approximately one foot more than United’s standard 31‑inch economy pitch and nine inches more than the airline’s Economy Plus product. In addition to the extended pitch, the seats reportedly include extra cushioning, a seven‑inch recline, leg‑rests, footrests and a privacy curtain.

Intended function as crew‑rest space

The article states that the primary purpose of these seats is to provide a rest area for flight attendants on long‑duration international flights. United allegedly designates the seats as crew‑rest accommodations, allowing cabin crew to lie down or stretch during extended duty periods. The presence of a privacy curtain and the location behind the main passenger cabin support this crew‑focused intent.

Occasional passenger access

Simple Flying notes that United sometimes makes the seats available for passenger reservation when the aircraft operates shorter sectors that do not require a dedicated crew‑rest area. In such cases, travelers can book the seats and enjoy the unusually generous legroom, effectively receiving a premium‑economy experience within the standard economy cabin. The article does not specify how often this occurs or the pricing structure for these seats.

Implications for travelers and airline operations

If the seats are indeed offered to passengers on select flights, they could represent a valuable option for travelers seeking extra comfort without paying for a higher‑priced cabin class. For United, the dual‑use design may improve aircraft utilization by allowing the same space to serve crew‑rest needs on long hauls and generate additional revenue on shorter routes. However, the lack of independent confirmation means passengers should verify seat availability directly with the airline before assuming access.
