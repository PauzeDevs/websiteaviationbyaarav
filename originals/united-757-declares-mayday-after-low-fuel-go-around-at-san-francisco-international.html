United 757 declares Mayday after low‑fuel go‑around at San Francisco International Airport

A United Airlines Boeing 757 on flight UA1023 from Boston to San Francisco aborted its landing due to an unstable approach, later reporting a fuel emergency with just over 5,000 lb remaining before landing safely.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-01 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

On the morning of 30 October 2026, United Flight 1023, a Boeing 757‑200 operating a Boston‑San Francisco sector, initiated a go‑around after ATC cleared it for Runway 28R. The crew cited an unstable approach and subsequently declared a Mayday because fuel reserves were approaching the aircraft’s minimum limit. The aircraft landed without injury after a 14‑minute diversion. United’s spokesperson said no formal emergency was declared, highlighting a discrepancy between radio communication and standard emergency procedures.

Incident Overview

According to Simple Flying, the aircraft was on final approach to Runway 28R at San Francisco International Airport when the pilots elected to abort the landing. ATC had warned the crew about wake turbulence from a preceding Boeing 787 and a slight tailwind. The flight began a climb at roughly 75 feet altitude, indicating an immediate decision to execute a go‑around. Shortly thereafter, the crew transmitted a Mayday call, stating a fuel emergency and reporting approximately 5,200 lb of fuel remaining.

Flight Details and Timeline

The flight, identified as UA1023, departed Boston Logan International Airport with 97 occupants, according to the source. After the aborted landing at 11:11 a.m. local time, the aircraft climbed to about 5,000 feet, declared the Mayday, and was cleared for a second landing attempt on the same runway. It touched down safely at 11:24 a.m., roughly 14 minutes after the initial approach. ADS‑B tracking data corroborated the climb and subsequent descent, while ATC audio captured the Mayday transmission. The aircraft’s registration was listed as N19117, a Boeing 757‑200.

Airline Response

Simple Flying reported that United Airlines responded to inquiries by stating the crew did not file an official emergency, despite the Mayday phrase being used on the radio. The airline confirmed the passenger count as 91 passengers and six crew members, a slight discrepancy from the 97 people mentioned earlier. United noted that the aircraft’s transponder code was not changed to the standard emergency squawk, which, while customary, is not mandatory if the situation is already communicated verbally.

Safety Context and Operational Considerations

The fuel amount cited—just over 5,200 lb—approaches the minimum reserve required for a Boeing 757‑200 under FAA regulations, though pilots retain discretion to declare an emergency based on operational judgment. The report did not establish a direct link between the wake turbulence warning, the tailwind, or any mechanical issue and the decision to go around. Simple Flying highlighted that the cause of the unstable approach remains unclear, and no further safety investigations were referenced in the source material.

However, the lack of an emergency squawk and the airline’s differing passenger count illustrate inconsistencies that merit further clarification in any subsequent investigation.
