Premium Economy Becomes the Most Point‑Efficient Cabin After 2026 Loyalty Program Overhauls

Frequent‑flyer analysts note that recent award‑chart revisions, especially Aeroplan’s June 2026 changes, have widened the mileage gap between business and premium‑economy, making the latter the smartest redemption option for most travelers.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-18 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that a series of 2026 award‑chart updates across major carriers have shifted the value proposition of frequent‑flyer points. Business‑class seats on trans‑Pacific routes have risen in cost by roughly 17%, while premium‑economy redemptions have stayed comparatively stable. The most striking example is Air Canada’s Aeroplan program, where a one‑way business‑class award in the 7,501‑11,000‑mile band jumped from 87,500 to 102,500 points, creating a steep barrier for families. As a result, premium‑economy is now viewed as the most mileage‑efficient cabin for international travel.

Loyalty Program Landscape in 2026

The aviation industry’s frequent‑flyer schemes have undergone a wave of devaluations, prompting carriers to raise the mileage price of business‑class awards while keeping lower‑tier cabins relatively unchanged. Simple Flying highlights that this trend is evident across legacy networks, where dynamic pricing and reduced saver‑level inventory have made it harder to secure a business‑class seat without a substantial points outlay.

Aeroplan’s June 2026 Award‑Chart Revision

Air Canada’s Aeroplan program serves as a flagship case. In June 2026 the airline adjusted its award chart, modestly lowering short‑haul economy points but sharply increasing the cost of long‑haul premium cabins. Specifically, the business‑class sweet spot for routes between 7,501 and 11,000 miles rose from 87,500 to 102,500 points for a one‑way ticket. The change inflated the cost of a round‑trip business‑class journey for a family of four to levels that often exhaust a typical mileage balance.

Premium Economy’s Emerging Value Proposition

Against this backdrop, premium‑economy awards have remained largely steady. Simple Flying notes that while trans‑Pacific business‑class awards climbed by about 17%, premium‑economy redemptions have not experienced comparable hikes. In some cases, premium‑economy seats have even become a more attractive upgrade over economy, offering a noticeable comfort boost without the steep mileage penalty now attached to business‑class. This stability has positioned premium‑economy as the “sweet spot” for travelers seeking value and comfort on long‑haul flights.

Implications for Travelers and the Market

The widening mileage gap influences booking behavior. Families and point‑rich travelers are increasingly directing their balances toward premium‑economy, especially on high‑demand routes to Asia. Airlines may respond by expanding premium‑economy inventory or enhancing cabin features to capture this demand. Simple Flying suggests that the shift could also pressure carriers to reconsider the pricing of business‑class awards if premium‑economy continues to siphon mileage traffic.

Key facts

  • Business‑class award prices on trans‑Pacific routes rose roughly 17% in 2026, according to Simple Flying.
  • Air Canada’s Aeroplan program increased a one‑way business‑class award from 87,500 to 102,500 points for distances of 7,501‑11,000 miles.
  • Premium‑economy award costs have remained relatively stable despite broader devaluations.
  • Family round‑trip business‑class bookings now often require a points outlay that exceeds typical balances, making premium‑economy the more viable option.

What this means

The evidence presented by Simple Flying indicates that frequent‑flyer devaluations are reshaping cabin preference hierarchies. By raising the mileage cost of business‑class seats while keeping premium‑economy pricing steady, carriers have unintentionally created a new value tier. This realignment benefits travelers who prioritize mileage efficiency and may drive airlines to further develop premium‑economy products to capture the resulting demand. However, the long‑term sustainability of this shift depends on how airlines balance revenue needs with the evolving redemption landscape.
