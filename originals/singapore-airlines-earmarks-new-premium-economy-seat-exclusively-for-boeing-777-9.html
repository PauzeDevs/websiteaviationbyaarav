Singapore Airlines earmarks new premium‑economy seat exclusively for Boeing 777‑9

The carrier will retrofit its A350 fleet with refreshed cabins while introducing a fully redesigned premium‑economy product on the upcoming 777‑9, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-11 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that Singapore Airlines (SQ) has decided to install a brand‑new premium‑economy seat, featuring a 16‑inch 4K screen and enhanced ergonomics, only on its future Boeing 777‑9 aircraft. The airline’s A350 fleet, currently undergoing a S$1.1 billion cabin upgrade, will retain the existing premium‑economy seat design, receiving a refresh limited to first‑ and business‑class cabins. The new seat is supplied by Collins Aerospace and is certified for the 777‑9 but not yet for the A350, suggesting a revenue‑focused rollout. No independent confirmation of these details is available.

Background to the cabin overhaul

In late October 2026 Singapore Airlines announced a multi‑phase upgrade of its long‑haul cabins under the next‑generation KrisWorld system. The programme includes a S$1.1 billion retrofit of 41 Airbus A350‑900 aircraft, primarily targeting first‑ and business‑class cabins. While the A350s will receive a refreshed interior, the airline has signaled a divergent path for premium‑economy, reserving a completely new seat design for its forthcoming Boeing 777‑9.

Why the 777‑9 gets the new seat

Simple Flying notes that the airline’s October 22 announcement, as reported by Executive Traveller and CAPA, indicated the new premium‑economy seat is certified only for the 777‑9 platform. The source suggests the decision is driven by certification constraints and a strategic focus on the cabin segment that yields the highest ancillary revenue. By allocating the most advanced seat to the 777‑9, Singapore Airlines can differentiate its flagship wide‑body from the A350s, which will continue to use the existing premium‑economy configuration.

Specifications of the new premium‑economy product

According to the Simple Flying article, the new seat will feature a 16‑inch (41 cm) 4K OLED display, Bluetooth audio, USB‑C power, a 40‑inch (102 cm) seat pitch, 9‑inch (23 cm) recline, and a 19‑inch (48 cm) width. Collins Aerospace is identified as the supplier, with design work attributed to Factorydesign. The report also mentions that not all 777‑9s may receive identical screen technology, implying a phased or tiered rollout within the fleet.

Implications for Singapore Airlines’ fleet strategy

The split‑cabin approach allows Singapore Airlines to spread its investment across two aircraft families while prioritising the cabin product that promises the greatest yield per seat. By retaining the current premium‑economy seat on the A350s, the airline avoids the cost and certification effort of a full redesign for that platform. The new seat on the 777‑9 could serve as a selling point for the aircraft’s launch, potentially attracting premium‑economy passengers willing to pay a higher fare for the upgraded amenities.

Key facts

  • New premium‑economy seat will be installed only on the Boeing 777‑9, not on the Airbus A350 fleet
  • Seat supplied by Collins Aerospace with a 16‑inch 4K OLED screen and enhanced connectivity
  • A350 retrofit focuses on first‑ and business‑class cabins; premium‑economy retains existing design
  • Certification for the new seat is confirmed for the 777‑9 but not yet for the A350

What this means

The decision to limit the next‑generation premium‑economy seat to the 777‑9 reflects a revenue‑maximisation strategy. Premium‑economy is a high‑margin product, and offering a differentiated experience on the airline’s newest wide‑body could command premium pricing and support the 777‑9’s market introduction. At the same time, preserving the existing seat on the A350s conserves capital and avoids certification delays, allowing Singapore Airlines to progress with its broader cabin refresh on schedule.
