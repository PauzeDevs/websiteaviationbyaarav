Irish Activist Charged Over Destruction of USAF C‑130 at Shannon Airport

Simple Flying reports that a 48‑year‑old Irish national has been indicted in the United States for sabotaging a Lockheed C‑130H Hercules, rendering the aircraft a $60 million loss.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-18 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying detailed the indictment of Daithi O'Corrain, who allegedly spent months planning and executing sabotage on a United States Air Force C‑130H Hercules parked at Shannon Airport. The activist’s actions, which included cutting wiring, damaging hydraulic and liquid‑oxygen systems, and contaminating fuel tanks, left the aircraft beyond repair. The report notes that the incident follows three lesser‑known events at the same airport in 2025, and that the case has not been independently verified.

Background to the Incident

Simple Flying notes that the presence of U.S. military aircraft at Shannon Airport has been a point of local contention. In 2025, three separate but less severe incidents involving U.S. aircraft were reported at the site. The latest episode involves a C‑130H Hercules that was stationed at Shannon awaiting a ferry flight to an undisclosed destination.

Sabotage Methodology

According to Simple Flying, the accused, identified as 48‑year‑old Daithi O'Corrain, allegedly conducted extensive reconnaissance and acquired a variety of tools before breaching the airport perimeter on 11 April. The report describes how he purportedly entered the aircraft through the rear crew door, disabled wire bundles and hydraulic lines, compromised a liquid‑oxygen line, destroyed cockpit gauges, and punctured air ducts. The narrative continues that he later accessed the roof via an escape hatch, struck the exterior with an axe, and introduced unknown substances and ball bearings into the fuel tanks. Simple Flying states that the cumulative damage rendered the aircraft inoperable and resulted in an estimated $60 million loss.

Legal Action in the United States

Simple Flying reports that the United States Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York has filed an indictment charging O'Corrain with destruction of U.S. government property, a felony that carries a potential ten‑year prison term. The filing, as described by Simple Flying, includes statements from U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald emphasizing the seriousness of the crime and its deterrent intent. The activist was reportedly apprehended after the aircraft’s crew spotted him upon returning to the plane for a subsequent flight.

Implications for Aviation Security

Simple Flying’s account highlights concerns about the security of military aircraft operating from civilian airports. The report suggests that the incident may prompt a review of perimeter controls and access protocols at Shannon and similar facilities. However, Simple Flying does not provide details on any official response from the U.S. Air Force or Irish authorities beyond the criminal indictment.
