Perth Ascends as Australia’s Primary Direct Link to Europe

Geography, modern twin‑engine fleets and strategic airport upgrades have turned Perth International Airport into the nation’s main gateway for non‑stop flights to the continent, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-04 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that Perth’s proximity to Europe, combined with Qantas’s deployment of long‑range Boeing 787‑9 aircraft, has reshaped Australian long‑haul travel. Since the inaugural Perth‑London service in 2018, the hub has added routes to Rome and seasonal flights to Paris, delivering significant time savings and revenue growth. The article notes that 2026 is projected to be the busiest year for Qantas’s Western Australian operations, though the analysis relies solely on this single source and remains unverified by other outlets.

Geographic Advantage Drives Hub Potential

The Simple Flying piece highlights that Perth sits roughly 2,000 miles (3,200 km) nearer to London than Sydney, placing it within the maximum range of contemporary twin‑engine jets. This location reduces the overall flight distance for Australia‑Europe trips, allowing airlines to bypass traditional stopovers in the Middle East or Southeast Asia and cut three to four hours from total travel time.

Fleet Modernisation and Route Launches

According to the same source, Qantas introduced a 236‑seat Boeing 787‑9 on the 9,010‑mile Perth‑London corridor in March 2018, marking the first non‑stop service of its kind. The success of that route prompted the carrier to add a direct Perth‑Rome flight in June 2022 and a seasonal Perth‑Paris service, expanding the hub’s European reach. Simple Flying cites internal Qantas data indicating that 2026 will see nearly 300,000 seats offered on these three routes, underscoring the airline’s confidence in the hub model.

Economic and Passenger Benefits

The article references reporting from The Points Guy that the Perth‑London flight proved both technically viable and highly profitable, suggesting strong demand for ultra‑long‑haul non‑stop services. By eliminating the need for a second‑leg connection, passengers enjoy shorter itineraries and airlines capture higher yields, a trend Simple Flying describes as a “high‑yield international hub” for Western Australia.

Future Outlook and Emerging Challenges

Simple Flying warns that the hub’s dominance could be tested by evolving airspace constraints and competing long‑haul technologies. While the article does not detail specific threats, it raises the question of how sustainable Perth’s exclusive position will remain as other airports and aircraft capabilities evolve.

Conclusion

Based on the information provided by Simple Flying, Perth International Airport has transitioned from a regional gateway to Australia’s principal direct conduit to Europe, driven by strategic fleet choices, geographic proximity, and targeted route development. The report, however, remains the sole source for these observations and has not been corroborated by independent outlets.

Key facts

  • Perth is about 2,000 miles closer to London than Sydney, fitting within modern twin‑engine range limits
  • Qantas launched a non‑stop 9,010‑mile Perth‑London service with a Boeing 787‑9 in March 2018
  • Direct services to Rome began in June 2022, with seasonal flights to Paris added later
  • Qantas projects nearly 300,000 seats on Perth‑London, Paris and Rome routes for 2026

What this means

The data suggests that Perth’s geographic positioning, coupled with Qantas’s adoption of long‑range aircraft, has created a competitive advantage over east‑coast hubs. By offering non‑stop connections, the airline reduces travel time and captures higher fare premiums, which appears to be driving both passenger growth and revenue. However, reliance on a single carrier and airport makes the model vulnerable to external pressures such as airspace restrictions, fuel price volatility, or the emergence of rival long‑haul routes from other Australian cities.
