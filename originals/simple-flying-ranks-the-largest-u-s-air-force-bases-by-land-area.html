Fairchild Air Force Base – 21 Square Miles

According to Simple Flying, Fairchild AFB occupies 21 square miles (56 square kilometres) southwest of Spokane, Washington. The base supports the Air Mobility Command and hosts the 92nd Air Refueling Wing, which operates a large fleet of KC‑135 Stratotanker aircraft. Simple Flying also notes that Fairchild houses the Air Force’s sole Survival, Evasion, Resistance and Escape (SERE) School, training roughly 20,000 students annually. Additional units cited include the 36th Rescue Squadron, the Air National Guard’s 141st Air Refueling Wing, the Joint Personnel Recovery Agency and an Armed Forces Reserve Center. The article frames Fairchild as a critical aerial refueling hub and a premier training site for personnel recovery.

Kirtland Air Force Base – 68 Square Miles

Simple Flying reports Kirtland AFB covers 68 square miles (175 square kilometres) in New Mexico, making it the second‑largest base in the list. The base is described as the home of the nuclear command and logistics arm of the Air Force Global Strike Command, a key component of the United States nuclear triad. Simple Flying also states that Space Force’s Delta 15 is headquartered at Kirtland. The installation employs about 20,000 personnel, including both military and civilian staff. Research functions are highlighted through the Air Force Research Laboratory’s Directed Energy Directorate and Space Vehicles Directorate, which focus on advanced laser, microwave and space‑based technologies. Kirtland supports more than 100 mission partners, among them the Department of Energy’s Sandia and Los Alamos National Laboratories, the Defense Nuclear Weapons School, and various Air National Guard and Reserve units.

Implications of Vast Land Holdings

Both Fairchild and Kirtland illustrate how extensive acreage enables the Air Force to co‑locate diverse mission sets—operational, training, research and nuclear stewardship—within a single installation. Simple Flying’s emphasis on the size of these bases suggests a correlation between land area and the ability to host large‑scale infrastructure such as runways, test ranges, and secure facilities for sensitive programs. The presence of the SERE School at Fairchild and the nuclear command elements at Kirtland underscores the strategic value of space and survivability training that benefit from isolated, expansive environments.
