Boeing Faces Continued Production Hurdles and Potential Workforce Reductions

As the manufacturer struggles to stabilize 737 MAX output, uncertainty remains regarding the scope of future layoffs.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-22 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Boeing is grappling with persistent production delays for its 737 MAX program, leading to missed delivery targets and ongoing workforce reviews. While the company has already announced a 5% reduction in its commercial airplanes division, management has declined to rule out further layoffs as it attempts to resolve critical supply chain bottlenecks.

Production Targets and Bottlenecks

Boeing is currently facing significant challenges in scaling its 737 MAX production. The company had originally aimed to reach a monthly output of 47 jets by the summer of 2026, a goal that has now been deferred to early 2027. According to Simple Flying, citing information attributed to Reuters, the primary obstacle is a shortfall in wing production. Chief Executive Kelly Ortberg noted in October that the wing assembly line has not yet achieved the stability required to support higher production rates, and anticipated process improvements have failed to yield the expected efficiency gains.

Workforce Uncertainty

The company has announced plans to reduce its commercial airplanes division workforce by 5%, affecting approximately 50,000 employees. Simple Flying reports that the Society of Professional Engineering Employees in Aerospace (SPEEA) has sought clarification on whether further layoffs are imminent. A spokesperson for the union stated that Boeing management could not confirm if the December 9 layoff notices would be the final round. Managers have indicated that all commercial sites are under review, with cuts specifically targeting work statements that are concluding or can be phased out, rather than implementing broad, across-the-board reductions.

Financial and Operational Outlook

The production delays have directly impacted Boeing's financial performance for the year. Chief Financial Officer Jay Malave confirmed that the company will miss its target of delivering 500 737 aircraft in 2026, though the exact deficit remains undisclosed. Looking ahead, Boeing has set a new target of 52 jets per month for the second half of 2027. However, leadership has emphasized that this goal is contingent upon first demonstrating sustained, stable performance at the 47-jet-per-month rate.

Key facts

  • Boeing is currently producing 42 737 MAX jets per month, missing its previous goal of 47.
  • The company plans to cut 5% of its 50,000-person commercial airplanes division workforce.
  • Wing production bottlenecks have been identified as the primary cause for the slowed assembly line.
  • Boeing will miss its 2026 target of delivering 500 737 aircraft.

What this means

The coexistence of strong market demand for the 737 MAX and significant workforce reductions highlights a structural inefficiency within Boeing's manufacturing process. Because the wing production line acts as a bottleneck, increasing assembly staff does not translate into higher output, rendering current labor levels unsustainable in the context of the company's inability to meet production milestones.
