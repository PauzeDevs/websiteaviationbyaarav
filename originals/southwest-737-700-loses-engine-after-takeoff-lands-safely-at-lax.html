Southwest 737-700 Loses Engine After Takeoff, Lands Safely at LAX

A 19‑year‑old Southwest Airlines Boeing 737‑700 experienced a loss of power on its right‑hand engine shortly after departing John Wayne Airport, prompting an emergency diversion to Los Angeles International Airport where the aircraft touched down without injury.

Incident Overview

According to Simple Flying, the Southwest flight departed John Wayne Airport (SNA) bound for San José Mineta International Airport (SJC) when the crew reported a loss of power on the aircraft’s number‑two engine. The pilots squawked 7700, the universal emergency code, and executed the emergency checklist for a single‑engine operation. Within minutes the aircraft was cleared for an immediate diversion to Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), where it landed at approximately 12:25 p.m. local time. All 97 passengers and crew were reported safe, and the aircraft came to a stop without further incident.

The aircraft involved, registration N281WN, is a Boeing 737‑700 that entered service with Southwest in 2007, making it roughly 19 years old at the time of the event. The flight was designated WN 3396 and was scheduled to operate a short‑haul route from SNA to SJC. No injuries were reported, and the airline indicated that passengers would be re‑booked on alternate services to complete their journeys.
