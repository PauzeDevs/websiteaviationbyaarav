British Airways Grounds A321neo After Double Tail‑Strike at Heathrow

A British Airways Airbus A321neo suffered two successive tail‑strikes during a landing attempt at London Heathrow on 13 Nov 2026, prompting a temporary grounding while investigations commence.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-13 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

On 13 November 2026 a British Airways flight from Amsterdam to London Heathrow experienced a double tail‑strike on its first landing attempt. The aircraft aborted the approach, climbed, and landed safely on a second try. The incident, captured on a livestream, led the airline to withdraw the aircraft from service pending inspections. The UK Air Accidents Investigation Branch (AAIB) has opened a probe, while the airline has limited public comment.

Incident Overview

During the scheduled flight BA431, an Airbus A321neo touched down on runway 27L at Heathrow and struck the runway surface with its tail assembly twice in quick succession. Video footage streamed live showed the aircraft’s rear contacting the pavement shortly after the wheels made initial contact. The crew elected to execute a go‑around, climbing to approximately 4,000 feet before re‑approaching for a second landing, which concluded without further incident.

Aircraft and Flight Details

The aircraft involved, registered G‑TNEB, is an Airbus A321neo that entered service with British Airways roughly three years ago and is one of twenty such jets in the carrier’s fleet. Flight data from Flightradar24 indicates the plane departed Amsterdam Schiphol at 10:45 GMT, later than its planned 10:20 GMT slot, and made its first landing attempt at Heathrow around 10:35 GMT. After the aborted touchdown, the aircraft completed a second approach and landed safely. No passenger injuries were reported.

Operational Response

British Airways removed the aircraft from its schedule pending a detailed inspection, describing the grounding as temporary with an expectation of returning to service by the following Saturday. The airline’s spokesperson confirmed cooperation with the AAIB and cited legal constraints on further comment. The incident quickly went viral, accumulating over 200,000 views on the BigJetTV YouTube channel, highlighting public interest in unusual landing events.

Investigation and Safety Context

The UK Air Accidents Investigation Branch has opened an investigation into the double tail‑strike. While the airline affirmed that the aircraft landed safely, the AAIB’s involvement suggests a focus on potential procedural, environmental, or technical contributors, such as runway conditions, approach speed, or aircraft configuration. Tail‑strike events, though relatively rare, can lead to structural damage requiring thorough checks before the aircraft is cleared for flight.
