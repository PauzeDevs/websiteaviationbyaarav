Turkey Finalizes $11 Billion Eurofighter Typhoon Acquisition

Ankara secures 20 new-build combat aircraft in a landmark deal, with potential for further expansion through second-hand acquisitions.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Turkey has officially signed an £8 billion ($11 billion) agreement to procure 20 new-build Eurofighter Typhoon aircraft, marking a significant shift in its defense procurement strategy away from a reliance on U.S.-built fighters.

A Strategic Shift in Procurement

According to a report by Aviation Week, Turkey has finalized a long-anticipated contract for 20 new-build Eurofighter Typhoons. This agreement represents the first major export success for the four-nation combat aircraft program since 2017. The deal signifies a notable departure for the Turkish Air Force, which has historically relied on a mix of new and secondhand U.S.-manufactured fighter jets. The new-build aircraft are slated for production at BAE Systems’ facility in Warton, England, with deliveries expected to commence in 2030.

Expansion and Second-Hand Potential

While the initial contract covers 20 new aircraft, Turkish Defense Minister Yasar Guler has indicated that the total fleet size could more than double. Ankara is reportedly pursuing the acquisition of an additional 24 second-hand Eurofighters, split evenly between Oman and Qatar. Should these secondary transactions proceed, it would result in Oman exiting Eurofighter operations entirely. Minister Guler suggested that the Qatari aircraft could potentially be delivered as early as next year, providing a bridge to operational capability before the new-build jets arrive.

Financial and Operational Scope

The total value of the deal is reported at £8 billion, with BAE Systems set to receive £5.4 billion for the aircraft and an integrated weapons package provided by MBDA. This package includes provisions for integration work, which may involve the incorporation of Turkish indigenous weaponry. The remaining £2.6 billion of the contract is allocated to other project requirements. The introduction of the Eurofighter is intended to facilitate the retirement of aging platforms within the Turkish inventory, most notably the McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom.

Key facts

  • Turkey signed an £8 billion ($11 billion) deal for 20 new-build Eurofighter Typhoons.
  • BAE Systems will receive £5.4 billion of the total contract value for aircraft and weapons.
  • Deliveries of the new-build aircraft are scheduled to begin in 2030.
  • Turkey is exploring the purchase of 24 additional second-hand Eurofighters from Oman and Qatar.

What this means

The acquisition of the Eurofighter Typhoon represents a significant diversification of Turkey's defense portfolio. By securing both new-build units and potential second-hand assets, Ankara is attempting to balance long-term modernization with the immediate need to replace legacy platforms like the F-4 Phantom. The inclusion of integration work for indigenous weapons suggests a strategic effort to maintain sovereign control over the aircraft's combat capabilities.
