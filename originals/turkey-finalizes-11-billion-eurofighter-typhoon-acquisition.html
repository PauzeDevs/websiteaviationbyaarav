Turkey Finalizes $11 Billion Eurofighter Typhoon Acquisition

Ankara secures deal for 20 new-build combat aircraft with potential for further expansion through second-hand acquisitions.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Turkey has signed an £8 billion ($11 billion) agreement to purchase 20 new-build Eurofighter Typhoons, marking a significant shift in its defense procurement strategy away from a reliance on U.S.-built fighters.

Strategic Procurement Shift

According to a report by Aviation Week, Turkey has officially signed a contract valued at £8 billion ($11 billion) to acquire 20 new-build Eurofighter Typhoon combat aircraft. This transaction represents the first major export contract for the four-nation Eurofighter program since 2017. The acquisition is notable as it marks Turkey's first purchase of a European combat aircraft after decades of primarily utilizing new and secondhand U.S.-manufactured fighters.

Expansion and Integration Plans

Turkish Defense Minister Yasar Guler has indicated that the current order could potentially more than double. The government is reportedly pursuing the acquisition of an additional 24 second-hand aircraft, with plans to source 12 units each from Oman and Qatar. Should this secondary procurement proceed, it would result in Oman exiting Eurofighter operations entirely. These additional aircraft are intended to facilitate the retirement of aging platforms within the Turkish inventory, specifically the McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom.

Industrial and Delivery Timeline

BAE Systems is set to manufacture the 20 new-build aircraft at its facility in Warton, England. The company is expected to receive £5.4 billion of the total contract value, which covers the aircraft themselves as well as a weapons package from MBDA and associated integration work for Turkish indigenous weaponry. While deliveries of the new-build jets are not scheduled until 2030, Minister Guler suggested that the second-hand aircraft from Qatar could potentially arrive as early as next year, allowing for an accelerated entry into service.

Key facts

  • Turkey signed an £8 billion ($11 billion) deal for 20 new-build Eurofighter Typhoons.
  • BAE Systems will receive £5.4 billion for the new-build aircraft and weapons integration.
  • The Turkish government is exploring the purchase of 24 additional second-hand aircraft from Oman and Qatar.
  • New-build aircraft deliveries are expected to commence in 2030.

What this means

The acquisition signals a pivot in Turkish defense policy, diversifying its combat fleet away from long-standing U.S. dependencies. By pursuing a mix of new-build and second-hand aircraft, Ankara is attempting to balance long-term industrial integration with the immediate operational need to replace legacy platforms like the F-4 Phantom.
