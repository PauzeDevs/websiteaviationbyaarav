United Airlines Accelerates Starlink Wi‑Fi Retrofit, Leaves Some Aircraft with Legacy Antennas

United aims to outfit its entire mainline and regional fleet with SpaceX’s Starlink by end‑2027, but the rapid pace has resulted in a mix of new and old inflight connectivity hardware on several planes.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-20 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

United Airlines announced an aggressive schedule to install Starlink broadband across more than 1,600 aircraft, targeting completion by the close of 2027. To meet that deadline, the carrier has begun installing the satellite system on some aircraft without first removing existing Wi‑Fi antennas, creating a temporary hybrid configuration. The move reflects United’s push to match premium rivals on connectivity, yet the mixed‑antenna situation raises operational and passenger‑experience questions that remain unverified beyond the Simple Flying report.

Strategic push for premium connectivity

United has positioned its inflight Wi‑Fi offering as a key differentiator in the U.S. market, where Delta is widely regarded as the premium leader. According to Simple Flying, the airline’s financial performance has improved in recent years, prompting a focus on product upgrades such as faster internet. The decision to adopt Starlink, a satellite service operated by SpaceX, follows United’s 2024 announcement that the carrier would replace legacy systems with the newer platform across its entire fleet.

Rollout timeline and fleet scope

The Simple Flying article states that United’s retrofit program targets over 1,600 mainline and regional aircraft, with a completion goal set for the end of 2027. The rollout began in 2025 after the September 2024 announcement. Initial effort concentrated on United Express regional jets, whose existing contracts were nearing expiration, before expanding to larger mainline types. The airline’s internal schedule calls for a rapid conversion pace, reflecting the scale of the undertaking.

Mixed‑antenna installations observed

In pursuit of speed, United has reportedly installed Starlink hardware on certain aircraft while leaving the older Wi‑Fi antennas in place. Simple Flying notes that this approach has resulted in a subset of planes carrying both the new satellite dish and the legacy antenna, a configuration that could affect weight, drag, and maintenance procedures. The article provides a snapshot of installation numbers for several aircraft types, showing, for example, 68 of 76 Airbus A321neo equipped with Starlink and only a handful of Boeing 777‑300ER fitted so far.

Implications for passengers and competition

If United succeeds in delivering the promised high‑speed connectivity, it could narrow the gap with Delta and other carriers that already offer robust inflight internet. However, the presence of dual antenna systems may lead to inconsistent service quality until the retrofit is fully completed. Passengers on partially upgraded aircraft might experience variable speeds or intermittent connectivity, though the Simple Flying piece does not detail any specific performance data.

Key facts

  • United targets full Starlink coverage of its >1,600‑aircraft fleet by Dec 2027
  • Retrofits began in 2025 after a Sep 2024 announcement
  • Some aircraft are being fitted with Starlink without removing legacy Wi‑Fi antennas
  • Installation progress varies by type; e.g., 68 of 76 A321neo now have Starlink

What this means

United’s accelerated retrofit reflects a strategic gamble: securing a market‑leading connectivity offering quickly, even at the cost of temporary hardware overlap. The decision to install Starlink before stripping out older systems suggests United prioritises schedule over operational neatness, betting that any short‑term inefficiencies will be outweighed by the long‑term passenger value of faster internet. Competitors may feel pressure to match the rollout speed, potentially reshaping the U.S. inflight Wi‑Fi landscape.
