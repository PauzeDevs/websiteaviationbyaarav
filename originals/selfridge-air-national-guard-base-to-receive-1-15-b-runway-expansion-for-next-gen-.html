Selfridge Air National Guard Base to Receive $1.15 B Runway Expansion for Next‑Gen Fighters

Michigan and the U.S. Air Force plan a multi‑phase runway relocation and extension at Selfridge, funded by $152 million state money and nearly $1 billion in federal grants, to accommodate F‑15EX and KC‑46 aircraft slated for 2028.

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that Selfridge Air National Guard Base in Michigan will undergo a major runway relocation and extension project to support larger, heavier aircraft such as the F‑15EX Strike Eagle II and KC‑46 Pegasus. The initiative combines $152 million from the state of Michigan with almost $1 billion in federal funding, and includes new taxiways, relocation of a highway and utility lines, and measures to reduce community noise. The article notes funding disputes and land‑owner compensation issues, and emphasizes that the base is one of five identified by the USAF for Cold‑War‑era runway upgrades.

Aging Cold‑War Infrastructure Meets Modern Mission Needs

The United States Air Force acknowledges that many of its airfields were built for propeller‑driven aircraft and early jets of the World War II and Cold War periods. While most installations have received incremental upgrades, the shift to fifth‑generation fighters and advanced bombers has exposed limitations in runway length, pavement strength, and taxiway geometry. Simple Flying highlights that Selfridge Air National Guard Base, currently operating a single 9,000‑foot runway (01/19), is slated for a comprehensive redesign to meet the operational envelope of the F‑35, F‑15EX, and future B‑21 platforms.

Project Scope and Technical Elements at Selfridge

According to the Simple Flying excerpt, the Michigan‑state‑funded portion of the project totals $152 million, while federal contributions approach $1 billion. The plan calls for relocating the existing runway, extending its length, and constructing two new taxiways that will improve aircraft flow and reduce runway occupancy time. Relocation will also shift the primary flight path away from nearby residential neighborhoods, a move projected to lower noise exposure for the community. Additional civil works include moving the Rosso Highway and rerouting local utility lines to clear the expanded movement area.

Funding Controversy and Stakeholder Concerns

The article notes that the financing structure has sparked debate. While the state allocation is earmarked for the runway and taxiway work, the bulk of the budget—nearly $1 billion—comes from federal grants aimed at modernizing the entire base. Local landowners have raised objections over compensation amounts for parcels required for the expansion, and environmental groups have expressed concerns—though the excerpt truncates specific details. These disputes illustrate the broader challenge of aligning national defense priorities with community and environmental interests.

Operational Impact and Future Outlook

Selfridge currently hosts A‑10 Thunderbolt II attack aircraft and KC‑135R Stratotanker refuelers. The introduction of the F‑15EX Strike Eagle II and KC‑46 Pegasus, both larger and heavier than legacy platforms, will demand stronger pavement and longer take‑off distances. By extending the runway and adding new taxiways, the base aims to sustain high‑tempo operations and support rapid deployment of next‑generation assets. The shift in flight paths is also expected to mitigate noise complaints, potentially easing future base‑expansion negotiations. Simple Flying frames the project as part of a broader USAF effort to upgrade five bases, though details for the other four installations are not provided in the excerpt.
