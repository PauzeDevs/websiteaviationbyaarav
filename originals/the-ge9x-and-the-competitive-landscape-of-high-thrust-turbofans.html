The GE9X and the Competitive Landscape of High-Thrust Turbofans

As Airbus evaluates a potential stretched A350, the GE9X engine remains a focal point of industry speculation despite GE Aerospace's apparent lack of interest in challenging Rolls-Royce's exclusivity.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-20 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

The GE9X, recognized as the world's most powerful commercial jet engine, currently powers the Boeing 777X exclusively. While its performance capabilities could theoretically support a future stretched Airbus A350, industry reports suggest GE Aerospace is not actively pursuing such an application.

Technical Dominance of the GE9X

According to Simple Flying, the GE9X is currently the largest and most powerful commercial jet engine in production. Guinness World Records has certified the engine for its performance during testing, where it achieved a maximum thrust of 134,300 pounds at an outdoor facility in Peebles, Ohio, in 2017. The engine's architecture features a 134-inch front fan equipped with 16 fourth-generation carbon fiber composite blades. Its design incorporates advanced materials, including ceramic matrix composites within the hot section, and maintains a 60:1 overall pressure ratio. While the engine is certified for 105,000 pounds of thrust for its service on the Boeing 777-9, these technical specifications establish it as a unique asset in the current aviation market.

Market Exclusivity and Potential Applications

GE Aerospace maintains a long-standing exclusive relationship with the Boeing 777 program, having provided engines for every variant since the 777-200LR. This exclusivity means the GE9X currently faces no direct competition in its specific thrust class. However, Simple Flying reports that the engine has become a subject of interest regarding the Airbus A350. Airbus is reportedly studying a stretched version of the A350 designed to compete in the 400-seat market, a segment currently occupied by the Boeing 777-9. Because a stretched A350 would require higher thrust levels than the current Trent XWB-97 engine provides, the GE9X is viewed as a potential candidate for such an aircraft.

Industry Outlook and Strategic Positioning

Despite the theoretical fit for a larger Airbus platform, Simple Flying reports that GE Aerospace has shown little appetite for entering the A350 market. Rolls-Royce has held an exclusive position as the sole engine supplier for the A350 family for two decades, and no competitor has challenged this arrangement to date. Consequently, while the GE9X possesses the necessary power to potentially enable a new, larger aircraft, the current commercial landscape remains defined by established supplier relationships and a lack of active interest from GE to expand the engine's application beyond the Boeing 777X.

Key facts

  • The GE9X holds the Guinness World Record for the most powerful commercial jet engine, reaching 134,300 pounds of thrust during testing.
  • The engine features a 134-inch front fan, which is larger than the fuselage diameter of the Boeing 737 family.
  • GE Aerospace currently holds exclusive engine rights for the Boeing 777X, as it has for previous 777 variants.
  • Rolls-Royce has been the exclusive engine provider for the Airbus A350 for the past 20 years.

What this means

The GE9X represents a significant technological benchmark in turbofan development. However, its market impact is currently constrained by strategic business decisions rather than technical limitations. The engine's potential role in a future stretched A350 remains speculative, as GE Aerospace appears to prioritize its existing partnership with Boeing over competing for a foothold in the Airbus supply chain.
