Japan Airlines Maintains Full-Year Profit Outlook Amid Rising Fuel Costs

Despite a 10.5% decline in second-quarter profits, JAL remains confident in its annual financial targets, citing robust international passenger demand.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Japan Airlines (JAL) has reaffirmed its full-year operating profit guidance, reporting that its second-quarter performance exceeded internal expectations despite significant headwinds from increased fuel expenditures.

Quarterly Financial Performance

According to a report by FlightGlobal, Japan Airlines recorded a second-quarter profit of Y57.4 billion. This figure represents a 10.5% decrease compared to the same period in the previous year. The decline is primarily attributed to a substantial 37% surge in fuel costs, which has placed pressure on the carrier's bottom line.

Strategic Resilience and Pricing Power

Despite the impact of rising fuel prices, the Oneworld alliance member has opted to maintain its full-year earnings forecast. The airline attributes this stability to strong pricing power within its international passenger segment. Furthermore, the carrier noted that its non-airline revenue streams have remained stable, providing a buffer against the volatility of the aviation fuel market.

Reporting Context

The financial data and strategic outlook provided by Japan Airlines have been reported by FlightGlobal. As of the time of this report, these figures and the airline's internal projections have not been independently corroborated by external financial auditors or third-party analysts.

Key facts

  • Japan Airlines reported a second-quarter profit of Y57.4 billion.
  • Quarterly profits fell 10.5% year-on-year.
  • Fuel costs for the airline increased by 37% during the second quarter.
  • JAL maintains its full-year operating profit guidance.

What this means

JAL's ability to maintain its full-year guidance despite a double-digit drop in quarterly profit suggests that the airline is successfully passing on increased operational costs to international travelers. The reliance on 'pricing power' indicates a favorable demand environment for long-haul travel, which is currently offsetting the inflationary pressure of fuel prices.
