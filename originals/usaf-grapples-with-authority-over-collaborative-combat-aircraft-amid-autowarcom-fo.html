USAF Grapples with Authority Over Collaborative Combat Aircraft Amid AUTOWARCOM Formation

FlightGlobal reports that the creation of the Pentagon’s new autonomous warfare command is raising questions about who will control the Air Force’s emerging Collaborative Combat Aircraft program.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-02 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to FlightGlobal, the U.S. Air Force is facing uncertainty regarding the chain of command for its Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) as the Department of Defense establishes the four‑star Autonomous Warfare Command (AUTOWARCOM). The new command is intended to centralise autonomous and uncrewed operations across the services, but its relationship to existing Air Force structures remains unclear, prompting concerns about operational control, funding, and integration with manned platforms.

AUTOWARCOM’s Mandate and Structure

The Department of Defense, led by Secretary Pete Hegseth, announced the formation of AUTOWARCOM as a four‑star combatant command tasked with overseeing autonomous and uncrewed warfare across the joint force. The command is expected to develop doctrine, allocate resources, and coordinate testing for a range of unmanned systems, from drones to advanced combat jets. FlightGlobal notes that the command’s exact placement within the existing combatant command hierarchy has not been fully detailed, leaving room for interpretation about its authority over service‑specific programs.

Collaborative Combat Aircraft Concept

The Air Force’s CCA initiative envisions a tightly coupled partnership between manned fighters such as the F‑35 and F‑15E and autonomous jet platforms supplied by General Atomics. The goal is to enable the uncrewed aircraft to perform high‑speed, high‑risk missions while remaining under the tactical direction of a human pilot. FlightGlobal highlights recent flight tests of the XQ‑67A, a General Atomics prototype, as a step toward operational integration.

Authority Ambiguity and Institutional Concerns

FlightGlobal reports that the emergence of AUTOWARCOM has introduced uncertainty about which entity will ultimately command the CCA fleet. Historically, the Air Force has retained control over its combat aircraft programs, but the new joint command could claim jurisdiction over autonomous capabilities. This overlap raises questions about budgeting, acquisition decisions, and the chain of command during combat operations. The article does not provide details on any formal resolution or timeline for clarifying responsibilities.

Potential Implications for Future Operations

If AUTOWARCOM assumes primary authority over the CCA, the Air Force may need to adjust its acquisition strategy and operational planning to align with joint directives. Conversely, retaining CCA control within the Air Force could limit the broader joint integration that the Pentagon seeks. FlightGlobal suggests that the outcome will influence how quickly the United States can field mixed manned‑unmanned formations and could set a precedent for other services developing similar collaborative platforms.

Key facts

  • AUTOWARCOM is being established as a four‑star combatant command to oversee autonomous warfare across the U.S. military
  • The Air Force’s Collaborative Combat Aircraft program pairs uncrewed jets with manned fighters like the F‑35 and F‑15E
  • FlightGlobal indicates that the command relationship between AUTOWARCOM and the Air Force’s CCA program remains undefined
  • Recent XQ‑67A test flights demonstrate progress toward operational integration of autonomous combat jets

What this means

The report underscores a classic inter‑service tension amplified by emerging technology. While the Department of Defense aims to streamline autonomous capabilities under a single joint authority, the Air Force’s historical control over its aircraft programs creates a potential jurisdictional clash. The lack of a clear command line could delay procurement, affect training pipelines, and complicate rules of engagement for mixed formations. Resolving the authority question will be pivotal for the United States to maintain a competitive edge in autonomous combat operations.
