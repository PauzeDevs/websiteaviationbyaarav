Delta Becomes Only U.S. Airline Flying Airbus’ Latest Wide‑Body Jets

Delta’s long‑haul fleet now centers on the A350 and A330neo, a position its domestic rivals have abandoned, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-01 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Delta Air Lines has positioned itself as the sole U.S. carrier operating both the Airbus A350 and A330neo, the newest generation of wide‑body aircraft. The distinction results from a mix of Delta’s long‑standing relationship with Airbus and the shifting strategies of American Airlines, United Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines, which each dropped or altered their original Airbus orders. The move gives Delta operational experience and flexibility for future international growth, though the analysis relies on a single, uncorroborated source.

Historical Context of U.S. Airbus Wide‑Body Plans

The Simple Flying report outlines that U.S. legacy carriers once entertained sizable Airbus wide‑body programs. American Airlines inherited a commitment for 22 A350s after its 2013 merger with US Airways, while United placed an A350 order in 2009. Hawaiian Airlines also explored the smaller A350‑800 variant. Over time, each airline revised or cancelled those plans, leaving a gap that Delta later filled.

Delta’s Current Airbus Portfolio

According to Simple Flying, Delta now operates two families of Airbus’ newest wide‑body aircraft: the A350 and the A330neo. The airline’s fleet includes both models in commercial service, a combination not found among its U.S. peers. The article notes that Delta’s upcoming deliveries of the A350‑1000 will further expand its capabilities, though exact delivery dates and numbers were not detailed.

Why Competitors Stepped Back

The source attributes the divergence to a series of strategic decisions, including corporate mergers, order cancellations and evolving aircraft requirements. American’s A350 order never materialised into deliveries, United continues to hold a problematic commitment for 45 A350‑900s, and Hawaiian ultimately chose a different aircraft path. These shifts cleared the way for Delta to deepen its Airbus relationship.

Strategic Implications for Delta

Delta’s exclusive operation of both the A350 and A330neo provides the airline with a distinct advantage in terms of pilot training, maintenance familiarity and route flexibility. The Simple Flying article suggests that this fleet composition could support Delta’s international ambitions, especially as the A350‑1000 enters service. However, the report does not quantify the impact on capacity, cost or market share.

Key facts

  • Delta is the only U.S. carrier currently flying both the Airbus A350 and A330neo, per Simple Flying.
  • American Airlines and United Airlines once had Airbus A350 orders but have not taken delivery of the type.
  • Delta’s future A350‑1000 deliveries are expected to enhance its long‑haul network, according to the source.
