San Francisco International Airport Braces for Potential Flooding as King Tides Approach Thanksgiving Travel Surge

National Weather Service forecasts near‑10‑foot water levels in San Francisco Bay; airport officials warn vulnerable seawall sections could be overtopped, threatening runway and taxiway operations during the holiday rush.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-23 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that the National Weather Service predicts exceptionally high tides and storm surge in the San Francisco Bay area between Tuesday and Thursday, coinciding with the Thanksgiving travel peak. Portions of SFO’s seven‑mile seawall are reportedly only eight feet tall, raising the risk that water could breach the barrier and inundate the airfield. Airport spokesperson Doug Yakel said preparations are underway, but uncertainty remains about the extent of any flooding and its impact on airline schedules.

Forecast and Tidal Conditions

According to Simple Flying, the National Weather Service (NWS) has issued a coastal flooding advisory for the San Francisco Bay shoreline, projecting water levels that could approach 9.9 feet (approximately three metres). The advisory cites a combination of unusually high king tides, a developing winter storm, and warmer ocean temperatures that together amplify the surge. The forecast window spans Tuesday through Thursday, aligning with the Thanksgiving travel period, which historically generates the highest passenger volumes of the year.

Vulnerable Airport Infrastructure

Simple Flying notes that SFO’s protective seawall extends for more than seven miles but is not uniformly elevated. Certain sections, particularly near the southeastern corner of Runway 28 Left and the northern ends of both primary runways, are reported to be only eight feet high. If the predicted tide levels materialise, these lower stretches could be overtopped, allowing seawater onto the airfield. The article does not confirm whether the runways or taxiways themselves would be directly flooded, leaving the precise exposure of operational surfaces uncertain.

Operational Implications

The potential for water intrusion raises several operational concerns. Simple Flying indicates that any flooding could disrupt aircraft movements, ground handling, and passenger processing during a period when airlines already operate at capacity. While the report mentions that the airport is preparing for the scenario, it does not detail specific contingency plans such as runway closures, flight diversions, or passenger re‑routing. Consequently, the extent to which airlines might need to adjust schedules remains speculative.

Preparedness and Response

Airport spokesperson Doug Yakel, as quoted by Simple Flying, affirmed that SFO is ready to respond to seawater ingress. The statement suggests that the airport has activated emergency protocols, though the article provides no further detail on resources deployed, such as pumps, barriers, or coordination with local authorities. The lack of granular information limits assessment of the airport’s capacity to mitigate flooding impacts effectively.

What this means

The convergence of a high‑volume travel period with an extreme tidal event creates a heightened risk profile for SFO. Even modest variations in sea level could exceed the height of the airport's lower seawall sections, potentially compromising runway availability. While the airport’s claim of preparedness suggests some level of contingency planning, the absence of detailed response measures in the source material makes it difficult to gauge the effectiveness of those preparations. Stakeholders should monitor real‑time tide data and airport communications closely, as any overtopping could cascade into broader operational disruptions during a critical travel window.
