TSA PreCheck Touchless ID expands to 65 U.S. airports, using facial‑recognition for faster security

Simple Flying reports that the optional Touchless ID program, launched in 2021, now operates at 65 airports with five major airlines, but travelers must still carry a physical passport and the rollout details remain incomplete.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-18 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

According to Simple Flying, the TSA PreCheck Touchless ID program replaces the manual ID check with a facial‑scan at participating airports. Initially piloted with Delta in Atlanta in 2021, the system grew to 28 airports by January 2026 and reached 65 airports by June 2026, involving Alaska, American, Delta, Southwest, United and Hawaiian airlines. Participation remains optional, and eligible passengers must still present a valid passport. The program’s broader impact on security efficiency and passenger experience is still being assessed, and the source provides limited data on final deployment and performance metrics.

How Touchless ID works

Simple Flying explains that the Touchless ID service allows enrolled travelers to verify their identity through a facial scan rather than handing a driver’s license or passport to a TSA officer. The scan is matched against the traveler’s stored biometric data and the boarding‑pass information, allowing the passenger to continue through the checkpoint without a manual ID check. The TSA describes the system as a "facial comparison technology for faster, more efficient identity verification" and notes that it does not replace the boarding pass itself; passengers receive an updated boarding pass that triggers the facial‑scan lane.

Rollout timeline and airline participation

The program debuted in 2021 with Delta Air Lines at the Atlanta hub, according to Simple Flying. By January 2026, Touchless ID was available at 28 airports and supported five U.S. carriers—Alaska, American, Delta, Southwest, and United. Hawaiian Airlines later joined the initiative. The source states that by June 2026 the network had grown to 65 airports, with a focus on cities hosting major football events, though the excerpt cuts off before completing that sentence. No independent verification of these figures is provided.

Passenger eligibility and practical requirements

Simple Flying stresses that Touchless ID is optional and not all travelers qualify. Eligibility is limited to TSA PreCheck members who also hold a valid passport. Even qualified passengers are advised to carry a physical ID, such as a passport, when traveling. The program currently works with participating airlines and can be activated through airline enrollment or Google Wallet, according to the TSA’s description quoted in the article.

Potential implications for the aviation sector

If the expansion continues as reported, Touchless ID could streamline the security screening process, reducing queue times and labor demands at checkpoints. However, the reliance on facial‑recognition technology raises privacy considerations that have not been addressed in the source material. The optional nature of the program means that a mixed environment of touchless and traditional ID checks will persist, potentially complicating staffing and equipment allocation at airports until broader adoption is achieved.
