Avolon Secures Record 140‑Jet Order for Boeing 737 MAX 10

Irish lessor Avolon announced a purchase of 140 Boeing 737 MAX 10 aircraft, the largest single‑order from a leasing firm, shortly after the model received FAA type certification, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-04 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reported that Avolon, a Dublin‑based aircraft leasing company, placed a landmark order for 140 Boeing 737 MAX 10 jets. The deal follows the FAA’s recent approval of the stretched narrow‑body and pushes Avolon’s total MAX 10 backlog above 240 units. Boeing’s senior commercial executive and Avolon’s CEO both highlighted the aircraft’s operational efficiency and its role in fleet renewal for airline customers. The order underscores growing lessor confidence in the MAX 10 after a prolonged regulatory saga, but the details remain unverified beyond the single source.

Regulatory Milestone and Order Timing

The Federal Aviation Administration granted type certification to the Boeing 737 MAX 10 in early November 2026, clearing the final regulatory hurdle that had delayed the aircraft’s entry into service. Within days of that approval, Simple Flying said Avolon confirmed a purchase of 140 aircraft, marking the most sizable order from a leasing entity for the model to date. The timing suggests the certification was a key catalyst for the transaction, allowing the lessor to lock in capacity ahead of anticipated airline demand.

Order Scale and Financial Context

According to the Simple Flying report, the 140‑jet purchase expands Avolon’s existing MAX 10 commitments to a total backlog exceeding 240 units. The article notes that 737‑series aircraft represent roughly 30% of all jets financed by lessors, and that the MAX 10 has seen a five‑fold increase in lessor orders in 2026 compared with the previous year. No monetary value or delivery schedule was disclosed in the source material.

Executive Perspectives

Boeing’s senior vice‑president for commercial sales and marketing, Brad McMullen, was quoted by Simple Flying as saying the deal enables Avolon’s airline clients to modernise fleets with aircraft that lower emissions and operating costs. Avolon’s chief executive, Andy Cronin, echoed the sentiment, describing the agreement as a means to support airline growth and fleet renewal for the next decade while reinforcing the partnership with Boeing. Both comments were presented as statements from the executives in the Simple Flying article.

Market Implications for the MAX 10

The order is portrayed by Simple Flying as a vote of confidence in the MAX 10’s commercial prospects after a protracted regulatory battle. By securing a large fleet from a major lessor, Boeing may gain a foothold for the aircraft in markets where airlines rely on leasing to manage capital expenditures. The report also highlights that the MAX 10’s popularity among lessors this year has outpaced that of the previous model year, suggesting a shift in leasing strategies toward newer, more fuel‑efficient narrow‑bodies.

What this means

The Avolon order, as reported by Simple Flying, signals that leasing firms are positioning themselves to meet anticipated airline demand for newer, lower‑cost aircraft now that regulatory barriers have been removed. By amassing a sizable fleet, Avolon can offer airlines flexible lease terms, potentially accelerating the MAX 10’s entry into service across multiple carriers. However, the impact on Boeing’s overall production schedule and the aircraft’s market share will depend on subsequent airline purchase decisions, which remain unreported.
