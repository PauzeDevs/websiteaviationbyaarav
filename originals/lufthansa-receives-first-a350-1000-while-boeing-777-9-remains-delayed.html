Lufthansa Receives First A350‑1000 While Boeing 777‑9 Remains Delayed

The German carrier’s 2023 A350‑1000 order has already yielded a delivery, arriving months before the long‑awaited 777‑9, whose entry‑into‑service is now slated for 2027, according to Simple Flying.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-21 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Simple Flying reports that Lufthansa, which placed its initial 777‑9 order in 2013, took delivery of its first Airbus A350‑1000 in November 2026. The A350‑1000 order, placed in 2023, was increased to 15 aircraft and will primarily serve Munich, while the 777‑9, still delayed, is intended for Frankfurt and to replace the airline’s 747‑400s. Lufthansa currently holds 20 firm 777‑9 orders with 24 options, alongside cargo variants of the 777‑8. The differing delivery timelines illustrate how the carrier is using the A350‑1000 to bridge the gap left by Boeing’s program setbacks.

Lufthansa’s Wide‑body Portfolio and Order History

Simple Flying notes that Lufthansa first committed to Boeing’s re‑engineered 777‑9 in 2013, ordering 34 aircraft. By 2019 the airline converted 14 of those to options, leaving 20 firm orders and 24 options for the passenger version, plus seven 777‑8F freighters for its cargo arm. The 777‑9 is slated to become the launch customer for the type, with an entry‑into‑service date now pushed to 2027 after multiple delays.

A350‑1000 Order, Expansion and First Delivery

In 2023 Lufthansa placed an order for ten Airbus A350‑1000s, part of a broader deal that also included five A350‑900s and seven Boeing 787s. The A350‑1000 order was later expanded to 15 aircraft. Simple Flying reports that the first A350‑1000 was delivered in November 2026, with the type expected to operate mainly out of Munich. Across the group, Lufthansa has ordered a total of 75 A350s, split between 60 A350‑900s and 15 A350‑1000s.

Strategic Implications of Staggered Deliveries

According to Simple Flying, the early arrival of the A350‑1000 allows Lufthansa to begin replacing older wide‑bodies ahead of the 777‑9’s entry into service. The carrier plans to use the 777‑9 primarily at its Frankfurt hub to supplant the aging 747‑400 fleet, while the A350‑1000 will complement Munich operations. This dual‑type approach reflects a strategy to maintain capacity growth and fuel‑efficiency gains despite Boeing’s program delays.

What this means

Based on Simple Flying’s reporting, Lufthansa is leveraging the timely delivery of the A350‑1000 to mitigate the impact of Boeing’s 777‑9 delays. By introducing the A350‑1000 ahead of the 777‑9, the carrier can begin modernising its long‑haul fleet, improve fuel efficiency, and sustain route capacity. The staggered rollout also suggests a risk‑mitigation posture: Lufthansa secures operational benefits from the A350‑1000 while awaiting the 777‑9’s eventual entry, preserving its plan to retire the 747‑400s without a service gap.
