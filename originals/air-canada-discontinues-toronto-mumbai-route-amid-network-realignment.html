Air Canada Discontinues Toronto-Mumbai Route Amid Network Realignment

The Star Alliance carrier confirms it will not resume its long-haul service to India, shifting focus as Air India prepares to take over the route.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-04 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Air Canada has officially cancelled its Toronto Pearson to Mumbai service, which previously operated via London Heathrow. The airline is restructuring its Indian operations, leaving the route to be serviced by Air India.

Operational Shift in Indian Market

Air Canada has confirmed that it will not resume its direct service connecting Toronto Pearson International Airport (YYZ) to Mumbai Airport (BOM) in the coming year. This decision marks a significant change in the carrier's international network strategy. According to reports from Simple Flying, which cites data from aviation analytics firm Cirium, the route was last operational in March 2026. The airline is currently in the process of reallocating its fleet, which previously utilized Boeing 787-8 and 787-9 aircraft for this specific connection.

Historical Routing and Logistics

The Toronto-Mumbai service functioned as a one-stop flight, utilizing London Heathrow (LHR) as a transit hub. This arrangement allowed Air Canada to leverage its existing infrastructure at the British airport. The flight path involved an overnight departure from Toronto, a midday connection in London, and an early morning arrival in Mumbai. Return legs followed a similar structure, with early morning departures from Mumbai to London, followed by an afternoon flight back to Toronto. Simple Flying reports that this route was operated as a fifth-freedom service.

Future of the Route

While Air Canada is withdrawing from this specific corridor, the route is expected to remain active under different management. Simple Flying reports that Air India is set to step in to serve the Toronto-Mumbai connection. Air Canada’s decision to exit the route is part of a broader restructuring of its Indian operations, allowing the airline to deploy its widebody aircraft to other parts of its global network. The account regarding Air India's future involvement in the route has not been independently corroborated by Aviation by Aarav.

Key facts

  • Air Canada has cancelled its Toronto-Mumbai service, last operated in March 2026.
  • The route previously utilized a stopover at London Heathrow Airport.
  • The service was operated using Boeing 787-8 and 787-9 aircraft.
  • Air India is expected to take over the route following Air Canada's withdrawal.

What this means

The withdrawal from the Mumbai route suggests a strategic pivot by Air Canada to optimize its long-haul fleet utilization. By exiting a fifth-freedom route that relied on a stopover in London, the airline is likely seeking to reduce operational complexity and focus on more efficient or higher-demand segments of its network, while allowing a partner or competitor to absorb the capacity.
