Second B-21 Raider Test Aircraft Takes Flight from Palmdale

Aviation Week reports that a second B-21 Raider entered flight‑test on Sept. 11, 2025, marking a key step toward full operational evaluation ahead of the program’s 10‑year contract anniversary.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-12 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

Aviation Week confirmed that a second B-21 Raider test aircraft lifted off from Palmdale Regional Airport on Sept. 11, 2025, joining the Air Force’s flight‑test campaign at Edwards AFB. The flight, captured on social media and accompanied by a Lockheed Martin F‑16 chase plane, fulfills a milestone a month before the 10th anniversary of the U.S. Air Force’s contract award to Northrop Grumman. The article cites Air Force Secretary Troy Meink’s remarks on accelerating mission‑system and weapons evaluations, and outlines the program’s production status, including two static‑test airframes and additional flight‑test units under assembly since early 2022. No independent confirmation of these details is available.

Milestone Overview

According to Aviation Week, the second B-21 Raider test aircraft departed Palmdale Regional Airport on Sept. 11, 2025, and proceeded to the Air Force’s flight‑test complex at Edwards Air Force Base. Witnesses posted video showing the bomber in clean configuration, wheels retracted, with a Lockheed Martin F‑16 trailing as a chase platform. The flight occurs roughly one month before the ten‑year anniversary of the original contract award to Northrop Grumman, underscoring the program’s adherence to its schedule.

Program Timeline and Production Status

The article notes that the first flight‑test version of the B‑21 lifted off on Nov. 10, 2023, establishing the baseline for the test fleet. Since then, Northrop Grumman has delivered two additional airframes that are earmarked for static and fatigue testing on the ground. Moreover, two more flight‑test aircraft have been in some stage of assembly since at least Feb. 2022, indicating a pipeline that supports continued testing and eventual low‑rate production. All B‑21s are assembled inside Building 401 on Site 4 of the Air Force’s Plant 42 complex at Palmdale, after which they must exit the public airport before moving to the secure area at Edwards.

Strategic Implications

Air Force Secretary Troy Meink is quoted as saying the new flight‑test aircraft will allow the service to “expedite critical evaluations of mission systems and weapons capabilities, directly supporting the strategic deterrence and combat effectiveness envisioned for this aircraft.” While the article does not detail specific systems under test, the presence of a chase F‑16 suggests a focus on flight‑characteristics, integration, and possibly weapons release validation. The timing of the flight, close to the contract anniversary, may be intended to demonstrate progress to stakeholders and maintain program momentum.

Next Steps and Outlook

Aviation Week indicates that the second test aircraft will join the existing flight‑test campaign at Edwards AFB, where further sorties will assess performance, reliability, and weapons integration. The delivery of static‑test airframes provides a parallel path for structural and fatigue evaluation, essential for long‑term service life predictions. With additional flight‑test units already under assembly, the program appears positioned to sustain a steady cadence of testing, though the article does not specify a projected date for full operational capability.

What this means

The second B‑21 flight‑test sortie represents a logical progression from the program’s inaugural flight in late 2023, expanding the test fleet to enable parallel evaluation of avionics, weapons integration, and airframe durability. By increasing the number of airworthy test aircraft, the Air Force can conduct a broader set of mission‑profile flights, reducing the time needed to validate the bomber’s full suite of capabilities. The public visibility of the flight, reinforced by official comments, serves both a transparency function and a reassurance to policymakers that the strategic bomber program remains on track toward its deterrence objectives.
