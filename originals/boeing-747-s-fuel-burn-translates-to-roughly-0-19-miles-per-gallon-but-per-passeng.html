Boeing 747’s Fuel Burn Translates to Roughly 0.19 Miles per Gallon, but Per‑Passenger Efficiency Looks Better

Simple Flying reports that a cruising 747 consumes between 3,500 and 3,800 gallons of fuel per hour, yielding about 0.18‑0.19 mpg overall, while passenger‑level mileage reaches roughly 65 mpg when fully loaded.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-26 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

A Simple Flying analysis quantifies the Boeing 747’s fuel consumption, estimating a cruise fuel burn of 3,500‑3,800 gph and an overall mileage of 0.18‑0.19 mpg. When the aircraft’s typical 364‑seat configuration is considered, the per‑passenger mileage rises to about 65 mpg, still higher than a single‑occupant car but lower than a fully occupied one. The piece also notes the fuel needed for take‑off, the aircraft’s maximum fuel capacity, and the weight of fuel burned on a London‑New York sector.

Cruise Fuel Burn and Overall Mileage

Simple Flying states that a Boeing 747 burns roughly 3,800 gallons of fuel each hour in cruise, a figure that aligns with a reported 0.9 gallons per second. The outlet also cites a BBC estimate of 3,500 gallons per hour, creating a modest discrepancy. Using the aircraft’s typical cruise speed of about 580 mph, the fuel consumption works out to roughly 0.18‑0.19 miles per gallon of fuel, meaning the jet travels just under two‑tenths of a mile for every gallon burned.

Passenger‑Level Efficiency

The article highlights that a 747‑400 operated by Qantas typically seats 364 passengers across three classes. When the total fuel use is divided by the number of occupants, the resulting mileage per passenger climbs to approximately 65.5 mpg. Simple Flying contrasts this figure with a baseline car efficiency of 50 mpg, noting that a two‑person car would achieve about 100 passenger‑miles per gallon, while a single‑occupant car would be less efficient than the 747 on a per‑passenger basis.

Take‑off Fuel, Capacity and Flight‑Segment Burn

Take‑off is identified as the most fuel‑intensive phase of a flight. Simple Flying estimates that a 747 requires around 5,000 gallons of fuel for the climb and initial acceleration, a number that varies with the aircraft’s weight at departure. The report also mentions that a typical London‑to‑New York flight consumes roughly 154,000 lb (70,000 kg) of fuel, and that the original 747 design could carry up to 48,400 gallons (183,000 liters) of fuel to sustain such consumption.

Key facts

  • Cruise fuel burn reported as 3,800 gph (Simple Flying)
  • BBC estimate of 3,500 gph creates a minor discrepancy
  • Overall cruise efficiency calculated at 0.18‑0.19 mpg
  • Passenger‑level mileage estimated at 65.5 mpg for a 364‑seat layout
  • Take‑off fuel requirement approximated at 5,000 gallons

What this means

The data suggests that while the Boeing 747’s raw fuel efficiency is low by automotive standards, its large passenger capacity improves the mileage per occupant to a level that can be competitive with a fully occupied car. However, the aircraft’s high absolute fuel burn, especially during take‑off, means that overall environmental impact remains significant. The variation between the 3,500‑gph and 3,800‑gph figures underscores the need for precise operational data when assessing efficiency.

What we could not verify

All figures are drawn from a single Simple Flying article; no independent sources were consulted to verify the numbers. The article presents two different cruise fuel‑burn rates (3,500 gph vs. 3,800 gph) without reconciling them, which introduces uncertainty into the calculated mpg. Passenger‑level mileage assumes a fully loaded 364‑seat configuration and does not account for variations in load factor, route length, or aircraft variant. No external data on actual airline fuel consumption or real‑world passenger occupancy was provided.
