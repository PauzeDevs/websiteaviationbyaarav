Evaluating the US Navy's Transition from C-2A Greyhound to CMV-22B Osprey

The shift from fixed-wing carrier-onboard delivery to tiltrotor technology prioritizes mission flexibility and engine transport over raw payload capacity.

AVIATION BY AARAV NEWSROOM · 2026-11-14 · 1 SOURCE · SINGLE-SOURCE ANALYSIS

At a glance

The US Navy has replaced its legacy C-2A Greyhound fleet with the CMV-22B Osprey, a move that sacrifices significant payload capacity for enhanced operational versatility and logistical integration with the US Marine Corps.

The Payload Trade-off

According to a report by Simple Flying, the US Navy’s decision to retire the Grumman C-2A Greyhound in favor of the Bell Boeing CMV-22B Osprey represents a notable shift in logistics strategy. While the C-2A Greyhound, a fixed-wing aircraft dating back to the 1960s, offered a higher payload capacity, the CMV-22B tiltrotor carries 4,000 pounds less—a 40% reduction in total cargo capability. Simple Flying notes that this transition initially appeared to be a strategic error due to the loss of raw lifting power.

Strategic Justification and F-35C Support

The primary driver for this transition, as reported by Simple Flying, is the specific requirement to transport the engine of the Lockheed Martin F-35C Lightning II. The C-2A Greyhound’s fuselage was not designed to accommodate this engine, and modifying the aging platform would have required an extensive and costly redesign. By adopting the CMV-22B, the Navy gained the ability to support its modern carrier-capable stealth fighters while simultaneously benefiting from operational commonality with the US Marine Corps, which already maintains a significant fleet of V-22 variants.

Operational Flexibility and Logistics

Beyond engine transport, the CMV-22B has fundamentally altered the logistics of Carrier Strike Groups. Unlike the C-2A, which required long, paved runways for operations, the Osprey’s tiltrotor capabilities allow it to land on smaller surfaces, including destroyer decks, beaches, and dirt fields. Simple Flying reports that this eliminates the need for intermediate transport, allowing for direct delivery of supplies and personnel from shore bases to various naval vessels, thereby reducing reliance on fixed-wing infrastructure.

Key facts

  • The CMV-22B Osprey carries 4,000 pounds less payload than the C-2A Greyhound.
  • The CMV-22B is capable of transporting the F-35C Lightning II engine, which the C-2A could not accommodate.
  • The transition allows for direct logistics delivery to ships without requiring traditional paved runways.
  • Operational commonality with the US Marine Corps simplifies maintenance and training for the Navy.

What this means

The transition from the C-2A to the CMV-22B reflects a shift in naval doctrine from high-volume, runway-dependent logistics to a more agile, distributed model. While the reduction in payload is mathematically significant, the Navy has prioritized the ability to support modern strike assets and the tactical advantage of vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) capabilities over the legacy platform's raw capacity.
